Q.1 Four-Bar Mechanism Equilibrium with Point Force (Winter-2024 Q.1a / Summer-2026 Q.1a - 10 Marks)
Question: In a four-bar mechanism ABCD, link AD = 500 mm is fixed. AB = 400 mm, BC = 1000 mm, CD = 750 mm. A force F = 80 N acts at point E on link CD at an angle of 150° to the horizontal. DE = 350 mm. Determine the input torque T₂ applied to link AB for static equilibrium.
Step 1: Equilibrium of Link 4 (CD)
Perpendicular distance from D to force F: h_E = DE ⋅ sin(150° - θ_{CD}) = 0.35 × sin(68°) = 0.3245 m
Overturning moment about D: M_D = F × h_E = 80 × 0.3245 = 25.96 N⋅m
Perpendicular distance from D to coupler line BC: h_D = 0.48 m
∑ M_D = 0 ⇒ F₃₄ × h_D = M_D ⇒ F₃₄ = 25.96 / 0.48 = 54.08 N
Step 2: Input Torque on Crank AB (Link 2)
Coupler force transmitted to pin B: F₃₂ = F₃₄ = 54.08 N
Perpendicular distance from fixed pivot A to line BC: h_A = 0.22 m
T₂ = F₃₂ × h_A = 54.08 × 0.22 = 11.90 N⋅m (Counter-clockwise)
Input Torque Required T₂ = 11.90 N⋅m
Q.2 Four-Bar Mechanism with Applied Torques (Winter-2024 Q.2a - 10 Marks)
Question: Four-bar mechanism: AD = 800 mm (fixed), AB = 300 mm, BC = 700 mm, CD = 400 mm. Torques T₃ = 30 N⋅m and T₄ = 20 N⋅m act on links BC and CD. Using the principle of superposition, find input torque T₂ on crank AB for static equilibrium.
Step 1: Response to T₄ Acting Alone on Link CD
Coupler force along BC due to T₄: F'₃₄ = T₄ / h_D = 20 / 0.38 m = 52.63 N
Torque on crank AB: T₂' = F'₃₄ × h_A = 52.63 × 0.24 m = 12.63 N⋅m
Step 2: Response to T₃ Acting Alone on Coupler BC
Vertical force at pin B: F''₃ = T₃ / L_{BC} = 30 / 0.70 m = 42.86 N
Torque on crank AB: T₂'' = F''₃ × (AB ⋅ cos 35°) = 42.86 × (0.30 × 0.819) = 10.53 N⋅m
Step 3: Total Input Torque by Superposition
T₂ = T₂' + T₂'' = 12.63 + 10.53 = 23.16 N⋅m
Total Input Torque T₂ = 23.16 N⋅m (Counter-clockwise)

Part (c): Core Mechanical Principles
1. Superposition: In a linear system, the total response produced by multiple simultaneous loads equals the sum of the responses produced by each load acting alone.
2. Virtual Work: For any frictionless mechanism in static equilibrium, total virtual work done during an infinitesimal displacement compatible with constraints is zero: ∑ δW = ∑(F_i ⋅ δr_i) + ∑(T_j ⋅ δθ_j) = 0.

Q.1 Horizontal Engine Dynamics (Winter-2025 Q.3b / Summer-2026 Q.3b - 9 Marks)
Question: Horizontal engine: bore D = 500 mm, crank r = 300 mm, connecting rod l = 1.2 m, reciprocating mass m_R = 250 kg. Crank angle θ = 60° from IDC. Net pressure p = 0.35 N/mm². Speed N = 250 rpm. Calculate: (1) Piston effort, (2) Connecting rod thrust, (3) Side thrust, (4) Crank effort.
Step 1: Kinematics & Gas Load
n = l / r = 1.2 / 0.3 = 4;   ω = 2π(250)/60 = 26.18 rad/s
Area A = (π/4)(0.5)² = 0.19635 m² ⇒ Gas Load F_L = 0.35 × 10⁶ × 0.19635 = 68,722.5 N
Step 2: Inertia Force (F_I) & Piston Effort (F_P)
F_I = m_R ω² r [ cos 60° + cos 120° / 4 ] = 250 × (26.18)² × 0.3 × [ 0.5 - 0.125 ] = 19,276.6 N
F_P = F_L - F_I = 68,722.5 - 19,276.6 = 49,445.9 N = 49.45 kN
Step 3: Component Thrusts & Crank Effort
sin φ = sin 60° / 4 = 0.2165 ⇒ φ = 12.5° ⇒ cos φ = 0.9763
1. Connecting Rod Thrust: F_Q = F_P / cos φ = 49,445.9 / 0.9763 = 50,646 N = 50.65 kN
2. Slide Bar Reaction: F_N = F_P ⋅ tan φ = 49,445.9 × tan(12.5°) = 10,962 N = 10.96 kN
3. Crank Effort: T = F_P ⋅ r [ sin 60° + sin 120° / (2√15.25) ] = 14,491 N⋅m
F_P = 49.45 kN | F_Q = 50.65 kN | F_N = 10.96 kN | T = 14.49 kN⋅m
Q.2 Petrol Engine Piston Effort: Small Bore Variation (Summer-2025 Q.4c - 8 Marks)

Find: (1) Net load on gudgeon pin, (2) Thrust in connecting rod, (3) Cylinder wall reaction, (4) Engine speed where these forces become zero.
Step 1: Kinematics at x = 10 mm
n = l / r = 200 / 50 = 4;   ω = 2π(1800)/60 = 188.5 rad/s
x ≈ r(1 - cos θ) ⇒ 10 = 50(1 - cos θ) ⇒ cos θ = 0.80 ⇒ θ = 36.87°, sin θ = 0.60
cos 2θ = 2 cos²θ - 1 = 2(0.8)² - 1 = 0.28
Step 2: Gas Force & Inertia Force
F_L = p × (π/4) D² = 0.7 × (π/4)(80)² = 3,518.6 N

Net load on gudgeon pin: F_P = F_L - F_I = 3,518.6 - 1,545.6 = 1,973.0 N
Step 3: Component Thrusts & Zero-Force Speed
sin φ = sin θ / n = 0.60 / 4 = 0.15 ⇒ φ = 8.63° ⇒ cos φ = 0.9887
Connecting rod thrust: F_Q = F_P / cos φ = 1,973 / 0.9887 = 1,995.5 N
Cylinder reaction: F_N = F_P ⋅ tan φ = 1,973 × tan(8.63°) = 300.0 N
For F_P = 0 ⇒ F_I = F_L ⇒ 1 × ω² × 0.05 × 0.87 = 3,518.6 ⇒ ω = 284.4 rad/s
Speed N_0 = 284.4 × 60 / (2π) = 2,716 rpm
F_P = 1,973 N | F_Q = 1,995.5 N | F_N = 300 N | Zero-Force Speed = 2,716 rpm

Q.6 Dynamically Equivalent 2-Mass System Derivation (Summer-2025 Q.3b - 6 Marks)
Question: State the conditions under which a body is replaced by a dynamically equivalent system of two masses. Derive the relation l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k².
Conditions: (1) Mass conservation: m = m₁ + m₂, (2) CG is unchanged: m₁ ⋅ l₁ = m₂ ⋅ l₂, (3) Moment of inertia unchanged: I = m₁ l₁² + m₂ l₂² = m ⋅ k².
From (2): m₂ = m₁ (l₁ / l₂). Substitute into (1): m₁ [1 + l₁/l₂] = m ⇒ m₁ = m [l₂ / (l₁ + l₂)]
Similarly: m₂ = m [l₁ / (l₁ + l₂)]
Substitute m₁ and m₂ into (3):
m [l₂ / (l₁ + l₂)] l₁² + m [l₁ / (l₁ + l₂)] l₂² = m ⋅ k²
[l₁ l₂ (l₁ + l₂)] / (l₁ + l₂) = k² ⇒ l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k²

Q.1 Critical / Whirling Speed Derivation (Repeated in ALL Papers - 7 Marks)
Question: Derive the expression for the critical/whirling speed of a light shaft carrying a single rotor with initial eccentricity e. Prove that critical speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations.

Step 2: Deflection Expression
m ω² (y + e) = m ω_n² y ⇒ ω² y + ω² e = ω_n² y ⇒ y (ω_n² - ω²) = ω² e

As ω → ω_n, denominator → 0 ⇒ y → ∞ (Resonance). Hence: ω_c = ω_n = √(k / m) = √(g / δ) rad/s.
Whirling Speed ω_c = ω_n = √(g / δ) rad/s

Q.4 Balancing Theory: Why Partial Balancing (Summer-2025 Q.11a - 4 Marks)
Question: Why is only a part of the unbalanced force due to reciprocating masses balanced in locomotives? State the condition to prevent hammer blow from lifting wheels.
1. Reason for Partial Balancing: Unbalanced reciprocating forces act along the horizontal line of stroke (m ω² r cos θ). To balance them, revolving counter-masses are added to the driving wheels. While these counter-weights balance horizontal forces, their vertical component (m ω² r sin θ) remains completely unbalanced, creating alternating upward and downward vertical forces (Hammer Blow). If 100% of reciprocating mass were balanced, the vertical impact on the track and wheel lifting would be severe. Therefore, only 50% to 75% (c = 2/3) is balanced.
2. Condition to Prevent Wheel Lift: Hammer Blow must never exceed static wheel load W: B_{rec} ⋅ ω² ⋅ b ≤ W.
