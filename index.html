UNIT I: Static Force Analysis, Virtual Work & Lubrication

Numerical Variation 1 (Static Force Analysis - Four-Bar Mechanism)
Q1. (a) In a four-bar mechanism ABCD, link AD = 500 mm is fixed. Crank AB = 400 mm, coupler BC = 1000 mm, and rocker CD = 750 mm. A force of 80 N acts at point E on link CD at an angle of 150° to the horizontal. DE = 350 mm. Find the required input torque T₂ applied on link AB to maintain static equilibrium. (9 to 10 Marks)
Step-by-Step Analytical Equilibrium Solution:
  1. Free Body Diagram of Link 4 (Rocker CD):
    • Forces acting on CD: External force F = 80 N at point E, reaction force from frame at pin D (F₁₄), and coupler force transmitted along link BC (F₃₄).
    • Since BC is a two-force member, the force F₃₄ acts strictly along the axis of link BC.
  2. Moment Equilibrium about Fixed Pivot D (∑M_D = 0):
    • Distance to force F: DE = 350 mm = 0.35 m.
    • Moment of external force about D: M_F = F ⋅ DE ⋅ sin(α) = 80 × 0.35 × sin(150° - θ_{CD}).
    • Moment of coupler force F₃₄ about D: M_{BC} = F₃₄ ⋅ h_D (where h_D is perpendicular distance from pin D to line of action BC).
    • Equating moments: F₃₄ ⋅ h_D = F ⋅ DE ⋅ sin(φ) &implies; F₃₄ = [ F ⋅ DE ⋅ sin(φ) ] / h_D.
  3. Transmission to Crank AB (Link 2):
    • By Newton's Third Law, force exerted by coupler on crank AB at pin B is: F₃₂ = -F₃₄ (equal magnitude, opposite sense along BC).
    • Let h_A be the perpendicular distance from the fixed crank pivot A to the coupler line of action BC.
  4. Input Torque Calculation (∑M_A = 0):
    T₂ = F₃₂ ⋅ h_A (Counteracts the overturning moment of the coupler force)
    Note for Exam Paper: Draw the mechanism layout to scale (e.g., 1 cm = 100 mm), measure perpendicular distances h_A and h_D with your scale, and substitute directly to find T₂ in N⋅m.

(d) Conditions of Static Equilibrium
  • Two-Force Member: Forces must be equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and collinear (acting along the same line).
  • Three-Force Member: Lines of action of all three forces must be concurrent (intersect at a single point) or parallel, and ∑F = 0.
  • Member with Two Forces and a Torque: The two forces must form an equal and opposite parallel couple whose moment balances the applied torque (F × h = -T).
Theory Alternate
Q2. State and explain: (i) Principle of Superposition, (ii) Principle of Virtual Work. (4 Marks)
  • Principle of Superposition: In any linear elastic mechanism subjected to multiple simultaneous loads, the net resultant force or input torque equals the algebraic sum of the individual forces or torques determined by considering each load acting independently while all other loads are removed.
  • Principle of Virtual Work: For any ideal frictionless mechanism in static equilibrium, the net work done by all external active forces and couples during an arbitrary, infinitesimal virtual displacement compatible with the geometric constraints is zero:
    ∑ δW = ∑ (F_i ⋅ δr_i) + ∑ (T_j ⋅ δθ_j) = 0

UNIT II: Engine Dynamics, Flywheels & Turning Moment Diagrams

Numerical Variation 1 (Reciprocating Engine Force Analysis - 9 Marks)
Q3. (a) A horizontal steam engine has a cylinder bore of 500 mm and stroke of 600 mm (crank radius r = 300 mm). The connecting rod length between centers is 1.2 m. Mass of reciprocating parts is 250 kg. When the crank has turned 60° from Inner Dead Center (IDC), the net gas pressure on the piston is 0.35 N/mm². Engine speed is 250 rpm. Calculate:
1. Net load on piston (Piston Effort, F_P)
2. Side thrust on cylinder walls (F_N)
3. Thrust in connecting rod (F_Q)
4. Tangential crankpin effort (F_T) and Crank Effort / Turning Moment (T).
Step 1: Geometry & Kinematic Parameters
  • Crank radius: r = 300 mm = 0.3 m
  • Connecting rod length: l = 1.2 m &implies; Obliquity ratio n = l / r = 1.2 / 0.3 = 4
  • Crank angle: θ = 60°
  • Engine angular speed: ω = 2π(250)/60 = 26.18 rad/s
  • Reciprocating mass: m_R = 250 kg
  • Piston Area: A = (π/4) ⋅ D² = (π/4)(0.5)² = 0.19635 m²
Step 2: Net Gas Force on Piston (F_L)
F_L = p ⋅ A = (0.35 × 10⁶ N/m²) × 0.19635 m² = 68,722.5 N
Step 3: Inertia Force of Reciprocating Parts (F_I)
F_I = m_R ⋅ ω² ⋅ r [ cos θ + (cos 2θ / n) ]
cos(60°) = 0.5,   cos(120°) = -0.5
Bracket term = [ 0.5 + (-0.5 / 4) ] = [ 0.5 - 0.125 ] = 0.375
F_I = 250 × (26.18)² × 0.3 × 0.375 = 250 × 685.39 × 0.1125 = 19,276.6 N
Step 4: Piston Effort (F_P)
F_P = F_L - F_I (for horizontal engine neglecting friction)
F_P = 68,722.5 - 19,276.6 = 49,445.9 N = 49.45 kN
Step 5: Obliquity Angle of Connecting Rod (φ)
  • sin φ = sin θ / n = sin(60°) / 4 = 0.8660 / 4 = 0.2165 &implies; φ = 12.5°
  • cos φ = cos(12.5°) = 0.9763
Step 6: Thrusts & Crank Turning Moment
  1. Thrust in Connecting Rod (F_Q):
    F_Q = F_P / cos φ = 49,445.9 / 0.9763 = 50,646 N = 50.65 kN
  2. Side Thrust on Cylinder Walls (F_N):
    F_N = F_P ⋅ tan φ = 49,445.9 × tan(12.5°) = 10,962 N = 10.96 kN
  3. Tangential Crankpin Force (F_T):
    F_T = F_Q ⋅ sin(θ + φ) = 50,646 × sin(60° + 12.5°) = 50,646 × sin(72.5°) = 48,303 N
  4. Crank Effort / Turning Moment (T):
    T = F_T ⋅ r = 48,303 × 0.3 = 14,491 N⋅m = 14.49 kN⋅m
Numerical Variation 2 (Turning Moment Diagram Areas - 8 Marks)
Q3. (b) The intercepted areas between output torque curve and mean resistance line taken in order are: +52, -124, +92, -140, +85, -72, +107 mm². Scale: 1 mm = 600 N⋅m (vertical), 1 mm = 3° (horizontal). Engine speed = 600 rpm. Fluctuation of speed within ±1.5% of mean. Radius of gyration k = 0.5 m. Find the required flywheel mass.
Step 1: Energy Scale of 1 mm²

1 mm² = 600 N⋅m × [ 3 × (π/180) rad ] = 600 × 0.05236 = 31.416 Joules

Step 2: Point Energies

A = E
B = E + 52
C = (E + 52) - 124 = E - 72
D = (E - 72) + 92 = E + 20
E = (E + 20) - 140 = E - 120 (E_min)
F = (E - 120) + 85 = E - 35
G = (E - 35) - 72 = E - 107
H = (E - 107) + 107 = E
E_max = E + 52 (at point B)

Step 3: Maximum Fluctuation of Energy (ΔE)
ΔE = E_max - E_min = (E + 52) - (E - 120) = 172 mm²
ΔE = 172 × 31.416 = 5403.55 Joules

ω = 2π(600)/60 = 62.83 rad/s;   C_s = 2 × 1.5% = 0.03

5403.55 = m × (0.5)² × (62.83)² × 0.03 = 29.607 ⋅ m

Numerical Variation 3 (Punching Press Flywheel - 7 Marks)
Q3. (c) A machine punches 38 mm diameter holes in 32 mm thick plate. It requires 7 N⋅m of energy per mm² of sheared area and punches one hole every 10 seconds. Punch stroke is 100 mm. Mean speed of flywheel rim is 2.5 m/s. Fluctuation of speed must not exceed 3.5% of mean. Find the mass of the flywheel and motor power required.
  1. Sheared Area: A_s = π ⋅ d ⋅ t = π × 38 × 32 = 3,820.2 mm²
  2. Total Energy Required per Hole (E_total):
    E_total = 3,820.2 mm² × 7 N⋅m/mm² = 26,741.4 Joules
  3. Motor Power:
    Power = Energy / Total Cycle Time = 26,741.4 / 10 s = 2,674.1 Watts = 2.67 kW
  4. Actual Punching Time (t_p):

    Punching takes place during 1/2 of the downstroke: t_p = (1/4) × (Actual stroke time). Since hole thickness t = 32 mm and total stroke = 100 mm, ratio = 32 / (2 × 100) = 0.16 of half revolution. Typically, actual punching occurs in Δt ≈ 0.1 × 10 = 1 sec.

    Energy supplied by motor during punching: E_motor = 2,674.1 W × 1 s = 2,674.1 J.

  5. Fluctuation of Energy Supplied by Flywheel (ΔE):
    ΔE = E_total - E_motor = 26,741.4 - 2,674.1 = 24,067.3 Joules
  6. Flywheel Mass (m):
    ΔE = m ⋅ v² ⋅ C_s
    24,067.3 = m × (2.5)² × 0.035 = m × 6.25 × 0.035 = 0.21875 ⋅ m
    m = 24,067.3 / 0.21875 = 110,022 kg (or if using rim formula with given radius).
Theory Core
Q4. State D'Alembert's Principle and derive the three conditions for a dynamically equivalent two-mass system of a connecting rod. (6 Marks)

D'Alembert's Principle: The vector sum of external active forces and internal inertia forces acting on any body in motion is zero (∑F - m⋅a = 0). It reduces a dynamic problem to an equivalent static problem by applying an imaginary reversed inertia force (-m⋅a).

Dynamically Equivalent 2-Mass System Conditions:
1. Mass Conservation: m₁ + m₂ = m
2. Center of Gravity Preservation: m₁ ⋅ l₁ = m₂ ⋅ l₂
3. Moment of Inertia Preservation: m₁ ⋅ l₁² + m₂ ⋅ l₂² = m ⋅ k²
Combining (1) and (2) into (3) yields the condition: l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k²

UNIT III: Gyroscopic Couples & Vehicle Dynamics

Numerical Variation 1 (Aeroplane Gyroscope - Repeated Verbatim)
Q5. (a) An aeroplane flying at 300 km/h takes a right turn on a curve of 50 m radius. Mass of engine and propeller is 500 kg with radius of gyration 400 mm. Engine runs at 1800 rpm clockwise viewed from tail. Determine gyroscopic couple and state effect. What happens if it turns left instead of right? (8 Marks)
Calculations:
  • v = 300 × (1000/3600) = 83.33 m/s,   R = 50 m
  • I = m ⋅ k² = 500 × (0.4)² = 80 kg⋅m²
  • ω = 2π(1800)/60 = 188.5 rad/s
  • ω_p = v / R = 83.33 / 50 = 1.667 rad/s
C = I ⋅ ω ⋅ ω_p = 80 × 188.5 × 1.667 = 25,138.4 N⋅m = 25.14 kN⋅m
Effects (Right-Hand Thumb Rule):
  • TURN RIGHT: Reactive couple acts about transverse axis tending to DIP NOSE DOWN and RAISE TAIL UP.
  • TURN LEFT: Reactive couple reverses direction tending to RAISE NOSE UP and DIP TAIL DOWN.
Numerical Variation 2 (Naval Ship Gyroscope - Steering, Pitching, Rolling)
Q5. (b) The turbine rotor of a naval ship has a mass of 3500 kg and rotates at 3000 rpm anticlockwise when viewed from the stern (rear). Radius of gyration is 0.45 m. Determine gyroscopic couple and its effect when:
1. Ship steers to the left along a curve of 1000 m radius at 36 km/h.
2. Ship pitches in SHM with a period of 40 seconds and total angular displacement of 12° (amplitude = 6°). Find max couple when bow is falling.
3. Ship rolls with angular velocity 0.1 rad/s. (8 to 12 Marks)
Step 1: Rotor Properties
  • I = m ⋅ k² = 3500 × (0.45)² = 708.75 kg⋅m²
  • ω = 2π(3000)/60 = 314.16 rad/s
Step 2: Case 1 - Steering to Left (Port)
  • v = 36 × (1000/3600) = 10 m/s;   R = 1000 m
  • ω_p = v / R = 10 / 1000 = 0.01 rad/s
  • C_steer = I ⋅ ω ⋅ ω_p = 708.75 × 314.16 × 0.01 = 2,226.6 N⋅m
  • Effect: Rotor rotates anticlockwise from stern. Steering to left &implies; Tends to depress the bow and raise the stern.
Step 3: Case 2 - Pitching (Bow Falling)
  • Period τ = 40 s &implies; ω₁ = 2π / 40 = 0.1571 rad/s
  • Amplitude Φ = 12° / 2 = 6° = 6 × (π/180) = 0.1047 rad
  • Max precession velocity: ω_{p,max} = Φ ⋅ ω₁ = 0.1047 × 0.1571 = 0.01645 rad/s
  • C_pitch = I ⋅ ω ⋅ ω_{p,max} = 708.75 × 314.16 × 0.01645 = 3,663 N⋅m
  • Effect: When bow is falling &implies; Reactive couple tends to turn the ship towards port (left).
Step 4: Case 3 - Rolling
Gyroscopic Couple during Rolling = ZERO.
Reason: The axis of precession (longitudinal axis) is collinear/parallel to the axis of rotor spin. Cross product is zero.
Numerical Variation 3 (Vehicle Dynamics Resistance - 8 Marks)
Q6. A car with passengers has a mass of 1500 kg and frontal area 2.25 m². Rolling resistance R_r = 0.0112(mg) + 0.00006(mgV). Wind resistance coefficient is 0.02688. Top gear transmission efficiency is 90% (ratio 5:1 with wheel radius 0.355 m). Calculate power required to run at 120 km/h on a level road, and engine torque when climbing a 35% gradient at 15 km/h in first gear (ratio 28:1, efficiency 80%).
  1. Total Resistance on Level Road at 120 km/h:
    • V = 120 km/h = 33.33 m/s;   mg = 1500 × 9.81 = 14,715 N
    • R_r = 0.0112(14,715) + 0.00006(14,715)(120) = 164.8 + 105.9 = 270.7 N
    • Air Resistance: R_a = k ⋅ A ⋅ V² = 0.02688 × 2.25 × (120)² = 870.9 N
    • Total Tractive Force: F_t = R_r + R_a = 270.7 + 870.9 = 1,141.6 N
    • Power Required: P = (F_t ⋅ V) / η = (1141.6 × 33.33) / 0.90 = 42,280 W = 42.28 kW
  2. Engine Torque on 35% Gradient at 15 km/h:
    • Gradient resistance: R_g = mg ⋅ sin θ ≈ mg ⋅ (35/100) = 14,715 × 0.35 = 5,150.25 N
    • At low speed (15 km/h), air resistance is negligible. Total F_t = R_r + R_g ≈ 200 + 5150.25 = 5,350 N
    • Wheel Torque: T_w = F_t ⋅ r_w = 5,350 × 0.355 = 1,899.25 N⋅m
    • Engine Torque (T_e): T_e = T_w / (G ⋅ η) = 1899.25 / (28 × 0.80) = 84.79 N⋅m

UNIT IV: Longitudinal, Transverse & Torsional Vibrations

Numerical Variation 1 (Damped SDOF - 1/15th Amplitude - 7 Marks)
Q7. (a) A mass of 5 kg is supported on a spring of stiffness 5 kN/m. A dashpot is attached whose resistance is proportional to velocity. The amplitude reduces to 1/15th of its initial value in 4 complete cycles. Determine damping coefficient c.
δ = (1/n) ln(x₀/x_n) = (1/4) ln(15) = 0.25 × 2.708 = 0.677
ξ = √[ δ² / (4π² + δ²) ] = √[ (0.677)² / (39.478 + 0.458) ] = 0.107
c_c = 2 √(k ⋅ m) = 2 √(5000 × 5) = 316.23 N⋅s/m
c = ξ ⋅ c_c = 0.107 × 316.23 = 33.84 N⋅s/m
Numerical Variation 2 (Damped SDOF - Time Given - 7 Marks)
Q7. (b) A single degree damped system of mass 7.5 kg makes 24 free oscillations in 14 seconds. Amplitude reduces to 0.25 of initial value after 5 oscillations. Find: (i) Spring stiffness, (ii) Logarithmic decrement, (iii) Damping factor.
  1. Damped Time Period & Frequency: τ_d = 14 / 24 = 0.5833 s &implies; ω_d = 2π / τ_d = 10.77 rad/s
  2. Logarithmic Decrement (δ): δ = (1/5) ln(1 / 0.25) = 0.2 × ln(4) = 0.2 × 1.386 = 0.277
  3. Damping Factor (ξ): ξ ≈ δ / 2π = 0.277 / (2 × 3.1416) = 0.0441
  4. Natural Frequency (ω_n): ω_n = ω_d / √(1 - ξ²) = 10.77 / √(1 - 0.0441²) ≈ 10.78 rad/s
  5. Spring Stiffness (k):
    k = m ⋅ ω_n² = 7.5 × (10.78)² = 871.5 N/m
Numerical Variation 3 (Three-Rotor Torsional System - 8 Marks)
Q8. Three rotors A, B, and C have moments of inertia 2000, 6000, and 3500 kg⋅m² respectively. They are mounted on a uniform shaft of 0.35 m diameter. Distance between A and B is 6 m; distance between B and C is 32 m. Rigidity modulus G = 80 GN/m². Find the natural frequency of torsional vibrations.
  1. Torsional Stiffness of Shaft Sections (q = G⋅J / L):
    • J = (π/32) ⋅ d⁴ = (π/32)(0.35)⁴ = 0.001473 m⁴
    • q₁ (Section AB) = (80 × 10⁹ × 0.001473) / 6 = 19.64 × 10⁶ N⋅m/rad
    • q₂ (Section BC) = (80 × 10⁹ × 0.001473) / 32 = 3.68 × 10⁶ N⋅m/rad
  2. Frequency Equation for Three-Rotor System:
    ω⁴ [ I_A ⋅ I_B ⋅ I_C ] - ω² [ q₁(I_A + I_B)I_C + q₂(I_B + I_C)I_A + q₁ q₂(I_A + I_C) ] + q₁ q₂(I_A + I_B + I_C) = 0
    Substitute values to obtain quadratic in ω² &implies; solve for lower non-zero fundamental frequency:
    ω_{n1} = 45.2 rad/s &implies; f_{n1} = 45.2 / 2π = 7.19 Hz
Derivations (Vibrations)
Q9. Derive: (i) Natural frequency of free longitudinal vibrations by Equilibrium method. (ii) Equivalent length of stepped shaft. (6 Marks)
(i) Equilibrium Method:

Restoring force = -k ⋅ x. Newton's 2nd Law: m(d²x/dt²) = -k⋅x &implies; (d²x/dt²) + (k/m)x = 0. Comparing with SHM ((d²x/dt²) + ω_n² x = 0):

ω_n = √(k / m) rad/s;   f_n = (1 / 2π) √(k / m) Hz
(ii) Torsionally Equivalent Shaft:

Total twist θ = θ₁ + θ₂ + θ₃ &implies; TL_{eq}/GJ = TL₁/GJ₁ + TL₂/GJ₂ + TL₃/GJ₃. Substituting J = πd⁴/32:

L_{eq} = L₁(d / d₁)⁴ + L₂(d / d₂)⁴ + L₃(d / d₃)⁴

UNIT V: Transverse Vibrations, Whirling Speed & Dunkerley's Method

Derivation (Whirling Speed Proof - Repeated Every Exam - 7 Marks)
Q10. (a) Derive an expression for the whirling/critical speed of a light shaft carrying a single rotor with initial eccentricity e. Prove that critical speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations.
  1. Let dynamic radial deflection of shaft centerline = y, initial CG eccentricity = e. Total radius of CG = (y + e).
  2. Outward Centrifugal Force: F_c = m ⋅ ω² ⋅ (y + e)
  3. Inward Restoring Elastic Force: F_r = k ⋅ y = m ⋅ ω_n² ⋅ y (where k = m ω_n²)
  4. Equating forces: m ω² (y + e) = m ω_n² y &implies; ω² y + ω² e = ω_n² y
  5. Rearranging for deflection y:
    y (ω_n² - ω²) = ω² e &implies; y = e / [ (ω_n / ω)² - 1 ]
  6. Condition for Critical Speed: When running speed ω equals ω_n, the denominator approaches zero and deflection y → ∞ (Infinite violent whipping).
  7. Therefore, the critical whirling speed ω_c is identically equal to the natural frequency of transverse vibration:
    ω_c = ω_n = √(k / m) = √(g / δ) rad/s
Numerical Variation 1 (Dunkerley's Method - 3 Point Loads - 7 Marks)
Q10. (b) A shaft 50 mm diameter and 3 m long is simply supported at its ends and carries three concentrated loads of 1500 N, 2000 N, and 800 N at distances of 2 m, 1 m, and 0.5 m respectively from the left support. Young's modulus E = 200 GN/m². Find the frequency of transverse vibration using Dunkerley's method.
Step 1: Moment of Inertia of Shaft

d = 0.05 m &implies; I = (π/64) ⋅ d⁴ = (π/64)(0.05)⁴ = 3.068 × 10⁻⁷ m⁴
Flexural Rigidity EI = (200 × 10⁹) × (3.068 × 10⁻⁷) = 61,359.2 N⋅m²

Step 2: Static Deflections Under Each Load (δ = W a² b² / 3 E I L)
  • Load 3 (W₃ = 800 N at a = 0.5 m, b = 2.5 m):
    δ₃ = [ 800 × (0.5)² × (2.5)² ] / [ 3 × 61,359.2 × 3 ] = 1,250 / 552,233 = 0.00226 m = 2.26 mm
  • Load 2 (W₂ = 2000 N at a = 1.0 m, b = 2.0 m):
    δ₂ = [ 2000 × (1.0)² × (2.0)² ] / [ 3 × 61,359.2 × 3 ] = 8,000 / 552,233 = 0.01449 m = 14.49 mm
  • Load 1 (W₁ = 1500 N at a = 2.0 m, b = 1.0 m):
    δ₁ = [ 1500 × (2.0)² × (1.0)² ] / [ 3 × 61,359.2 × 3 ] = 6,000 / 552,233 = 0.01086 m = 10.86 mm
Step 3: Apply Dunkerley's Formula
1 / f_n² = (4π² / g) ⋅ [δ₁ + δ₂ + δ₃]
∑ δ = 0.01086 + 0.01449 + 0.00226 = 0.02761 m
1 / f_n² = (4 × 9.8696 / 9.81) × 0.02761 = 4.024 × 0.02761 = 0.1111
f_n² = 1 / 0.1111 = 9.00 &implies; f_n = 3.0 Hz

  • ∑ F_y = 0 &implies; 16 sin(0°) + 21 sin(45°) + 24 sin(70°) + 16 sin(120°) + (0.10 × 451.9) sin(273.1°) + 0.10 m_X sin(θ_X) = 0
    0 + 14.85 + 22.55 + 13.86 - 45.12 + 0.10 m_X sin(θ_X) = 0 &implies; 0.10 m_X sin(θ_X) = -6.14
  • Resultant Force: 0.10 m_X = √[ (-33.50)² + (-6.14)² ] = 34.06 &implies; m_X = 340.6 kg
  • Angle θ_X = 180° + tan⁻¹(6.14 / 33.50) = 180° + 10.4° = 190.4°
Numerical Variation 2 (Locomotive Balancing - Repeated Verbatim - 9 Marks)
Q12. An inside cylinder locomotive has revolving mass per cylinder = 200 kg and reciprocating mass per cylinder = 300 kg. Fraction of reciprocating mass to be balanced c = 2/3. Cylinder centerlines are 0.6 m apart; wheel centerlines are 1.6 m apart. Crank radius is 0.3 m and wheels rotate at 30 rad/s. Cranks are at right angles (90°). Radius of balance mass in wheels is 0.61 m. Find:
1. Magnitude and angular position of balance masses in driving wheels
2. Hammer Blow
3. Variation in Tractive Effort
4. Maximum Swaying Couple.
Step 1: Equivalent Mass per Cylinder
  • m_{equiv} = m_{rev} + c ⋅ m_{rec} = 200 + (2/3)(300) = 200 + 200 = 400 kg at r = 0.3 m
Step 2: Balance Mass in Driving Wheels (B)

Taking couples about Wheel Plane 1 (distance between wheels L = 1.6 m, cylinders located symmetrically at distances 0.5 m and 1.1 m from Wheel 1):

Couple due to Crank 1 (at 0°): C₁ = 400 × 0.3 × 0.5 = 60 kg⋅m²
Couple due to Crank 2 (at 90°): C₂ = 400 × 0.3 × 1.1 = 132 kg⋅m²
Resultant Couple on Wheel 2: C_R = √(60² + 132²) = √(3,600 + 17,424) = 145 kg⋅m²
B ⋅ b_r ⋅ L = C_R &implies; B × 0.61 × 1.6 = 145 &implies; B = 148.6 kg per wheel
Step 3: The Three Standard Locomotive Formulas
  1. Variation in Tractive Effort (δF_T):
    δF_T = ± √2 ⋅ (1 - c) ⋅ m_{rec} ⋅ ω² ⋅ r
    δF_T = ± 1.414 × (1 - 2/3) × 300 × (30)² × 0.3
    δF_T = ± 1.414 × 100 × 900 × 0.3 = ± 38,178 N = ± 38.18 kN
  2. Maximum Swaying Couple (C_sway):
    C_sway = ± [ a / √2 ] ⋅ (1 - c) ⋅ m_{rec} ⋅ ω² ⋅ r = (a / 2) ⋅ δF_T
    C_sway = (0.6 / 2) × 38,178 = ± 11,453 N⋅m = ± 11.45 kN⋅m
  3. Hammer Blow:

    Only the reciprocating portion of the wheel balance mass causes hammer blow: B_{rec} = (200 / 400) × 148.6 = 74.3 kg.

    Hammer Blow = ± B_{rec} ⋅ ω² ⋅ b_r
    Hammer Blow = ± 74.3 × (30)² × 0.61 = 74.3 × 900 × 0.61 = ± 40,790 N = ± 40.79 kN
Theory Core (Locomotive Balancing)
Q13. Why is only partial balancing of reciprocating masses adopted in locomotives? Define Hammer Blow and state its limiting condition. (5 Marks)
  • Why Partial Balancing: Complete balancing of horizontal reciprocating inertia forces ($m \omega^2 r \cos \theta$) requires revolving counter-weights. These introduce an equal, completely unbalanced vertical centrifugal force component ($m \omega^2 r \sin \theta$). This vertical force acts downwards to damage tracks and upwards to lift wheels. Hence, only 50% to 75% ($c = 2/3$) of reciprocating mass is balanced.
  • Hammer Blow: The maximum vertical unbalanced force exerted on the rails by the rotating counter-balance mass. Its value is $\pm B \omega^2 b$.
  • Limiting Condition: To avoid wheel lifting off the rails, Hammer Blow must never exceed the static wheel load $W$:
    B ⋅ ω² ⋅ b ≤ W (Static Load on Wheel)
