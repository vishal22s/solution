Q.1 Journal Bearing for Centrifugal Pump: 20 kN, 900 rpm (Summer-2026 Q.7a / Summer-2025 Q.7b — 15 Marks)
Question: Design a journal bearing for a centrifugal pump from the following data:
Load on journal = 20 kN; Speed of journal = 900 r.p.m.; Atmospheric temperature of oil = 15.5°C; Absolute viscosity of oil at 55°C = 0.017 kg/m-s; Operating temperature of oil = 55°C; Diameter of Journal = 100 mm; Clearance ratio = 0.0013; Heat dissipation coefficient = 1232 W/m²/°C; Maximum bearing pressure = 1.5 N/mm². Calculate also mass of lubricating oil required for artificial cooling, if rise of temperature of oil be limited to 10°C. Take specific heat of oil = 1900 J/kg/°C.
Step 1: Length of Bearing ($l$) and Pressure Verification ($p$)
Choose standard length-to-diameter ratio for centrifugal pumps: $\frac{l}{d} = 1.6$
$$l = 1.6 \times d = 1.6 \times 100 = \mathbf{160\text{ mm} = 0.16\text{ m}}$$ Actual operating bearing pressure: $$p = \frac{W}{l \times d} = \frac{20,000}{160 \times 100} = \mathbf{1.25\text{ N/mm}^2}$$ Since $p = 1.25\text{ N/mm}^2 < p_{\text{max}} = 1.5\text{ N/mm}^2$, the chosen size is SAFE.
Step 2: Coefficient of Friction ($\mu$) (Data Book Page 72)
$$\mu = \frac{33}{10^8} \left(\frac{Z \cdot N}{p}\right) \left(\frac{d}{c}\right) + k$$ Given: $\frac{c}{d} = 0.0013 \implies \frac{d}{c} = \frac{1}{0.0013} = 769.23$
For $\frac{l}{d} = 1.6$, end leakage factor $k = 0.002$
Characteristic number: $\frac{Z \cdot N}{p} = \frac{0.017 \times 900}{1.25} = 12.24$
$$\mu = \frac{33}{10^8} \times (12.24) \times (769.23) + 0.002 = 0.003107 + 0.002 = \mathbf{0.00511}$$
Step 3: Heat Generated ($Q_g$) (Page 72)
Rubbing velocity ($V$): $$V = \frac{\pi \cdot d \cdot N}{60,000} = \frac{\pi \times 100 \times 900}{60,000} = \mathbf{4.712\text{ m/s}}$$ Heat generated: $$Q_g = \mu \cdot W \cdot V = 0.00511 \times 20,000 \times 4.712 = \mathbf{481.6\text{ Watts}}$$
Step 4: Heat Dissipated ($Q_d$) (Page 72)
Projected area ($A$ in $\text{m}^2$): $$A = \frac{l \times d}{10^6} = \frac{160 \times 100}{10^6} = \mathbf{0.016\text{ m}^2}$$ Bearing surface temperature rise $(t_b - t_a)$: $$(t_b - t_a) = \frac{1}{2}(t_o - t_a) = \frac{1}{2}(55 - 15.5) = \mathbf{19.75^\circ\text{C}}$$ Heat naturally dissipated: $$Q_d = C \cdot A \cdot (t_b - t_a) = 1232 \times 0.016 \times 19.75 = \mathbf{389.3\text{ Watts}}$$
Step 5: Artificial Cooling Requirement ($m$)
Since $Q_g (481.6\text{ W}) > Q_d (389.3\text{ W})$, artificial cooling is mandatory.
Excess heat to be removed: $$Q_a = Q_g - Q_d = 481.6 - 389.3 = \mathbf{92.3\text{ Watts}}$$ Mass flow rate of cooling oil required: $$m = \frac{Q_a}{S \cdot \Delta t} = \frac{92.3}{1900 \times 10} = \mathbf{0.00486\text{ kg/s} = 0.291\text{ kg/min}}$$
Q_g = 481.6 W | Q_d = 389.3 W | Oil Flow m = 0.291 kg/min
Q.2 Journal Bearing: 12000 N, 1500 rpm, Viscosity in Centipoise (Winter-2025 Q.7a — 15 Marks)
Question: Design a suitable journal bearing for a centrifugal pump from available data:
Load on bearing = 12000 N; Diameter of journal = 70 mm; Speed = 1500 rpm; Atmospheric temperature = 30°C; Operating temperature of oil = 60°C; Viscosity of oil at 70°C is 22 centipoises; Specific heat of oil = 1800 J/kg/°C. Determine: (1) Amount of cooling required, (2) Amount of cooling required to temp rise 10°C, (3) Oil film thickness when no side leakage. Bearing is unventilated and projected to air.
Step 1: Length and Pressure
Assuming standard $\frac{l}{d} = 1.5$ for centrifugal pumps: $$l = 1.5 \times d = 1.5 \times 70 = \mathbf{105\text{ mm} = 0.105\text{ m}}$$ Actual bearing pressure ($p$): $$p = \frac{W}{l \times d} = \frac{12,000}{105 \times 70} = \mathbf{1.633\text{ N/mm}^2}$$
Step 2: Friction Factor ($\mu$) (Page 72)
Viscosity conversion from centipoise (Page 72 pencil note): $$Z = 22\text{ centipoises} = 22 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg/(m}\cdot\text{s)} = \mathbf{0.022\text{ kg/(m}\cdot\text{s)}}$$ Standard clearance ratio from Table 14.2: $\frac{c}{d} = 0.001 \implies \frac{d}{c} = 1000$
Characteristic number: $\frac{Z \cdot N}{p} = \frac{0.022 \times 1500}{1.633} = 20.21$
For $\frac{l}{d} = 1.5$, end leakage factor $k = 0.002$
$$\mu = \frac{33}{10^8}(20.21)(1000) + 0.002 = 0.00667 + 0.002 = \mathbf{0.00867}$$
Step 3: Heat Balance & Cooling Required
Rubbing velocity: $$V = \frac{\pi \cdot d \cdot N}{60,000} = \frac{\pi \times 70 \times 1500}{60,000} = \mathbf{5.498\text{ m/s}}$$ Heat generated: $$Q_g = \mu \cdot W \cdot V = 0.00867 \times 12,000 \times 5.498 = \mathbf{572.0\text{ Watts}}$$ Projected area: $A = \frac{105 \times 70}{10^6} = 0.00735\text{ m}^2$
Temperature difference: $(t_b - t_a) = \frac{1}{2}(60 - 30) = 15^\circ\text{C}$
For unventilated bearing in still air: $C = 280\text{ W/m}^2/^\circ\text{C}$ (Page 71 pencil note)
$$Q_d = 280 \times 0.00735 \times 15 = \mathbf{30.87\text{ Watts}}$$ (Note: If assumed ventilated with $C = 1232$, $Q_d = 1232 \times 0.00735 \times 15 = 135.8\text{ W}$.)
Amount of cooling required: $$Q_a = Q_g - Q_d = 572.0 - 30.87 = \mathbf{541.13\text{ Watts}}$$
Step 4: Mass of Oil Required for 10°C Rise
$$m = \frac{Q_a}{S \cdot \Delta t} = \frac{541.13}{1800 \times 10} = \mathbf{0.0301\text{ kg/s} = 1.80\text{ kg/min}}$$
Step 5: Minimum Oil Film Thickness ($h_o$)
Diametral clearance: $c = 0.001 \times d = 0.001 \times 70 = 0.07\text{ mm}$
Radial clearance: $c_1 = \frac{c}{2} = 0.035\text{ mm}$
Sommerfeld number: $$S = \left(\frac{d}{c}\right)^2 \left(\frac{Z \cdot n_s}{p}\right) = (1000)^2 \times \frac{0.022 \times (1500/60)}{1.633 \times 10^6} = 0.337$$ For $S = 0.337$ and $l/d = 1.5$, eccentricity ratio $\epsilon \approx 0.6$
$$h_o = c_1 (1 - \epsilon) = 0.035(1 - 0.6) = \mathbf{0.014\text{ mm} = 14\text{ }\mu\text{m}}$$
Q_a = 541.1 W | m = 1.80 kg/min | h_o = 0.014 mm
Q.3 Systematic Design of Journal Bearing: 10 kN, 1200 rpm, d = 60 mm (15 Marks)
Question: Following data is given for a journal bearing:
Radial load = 10 kN; Speed = 1200 rpm; Diameter of journal = 60 mm; Atmospheric Temperature of oil = 16°C; Operating Temperature of oil = 60°C; Viscosity of oil at 60°C = 0.020 kg/m-s. Give systematic design of bearing.
Step 1: Length and Pressure
Assume $\frac{l}{d} = 1.5$: $$l = 1.5 \times 60 = \mathbf{90\text{ mm} = 0.09\text{ m}}$$ Operating bearing pressure: $$p = \frac{W}{l \times d} = \frac{10,000}{90 \times 60} = \mathbf{1.852\text{ N/mm}^2}$$
Step 2: Friction Factor ($\mu$) (Page 72)
Assume clearance ratio: $\frac{c}{d} = 0.001 \implies \frac{d}{c} = 1000$
Characteristic number: $\frac{Z \cdot N}{p} = \frac{0.020 \times 1200}{1.852} = 12.96$
End leakage factor: $k = 0.002$
$$\mu = \frac{33}{10^8}(12.96)(1000) + 0.002 = 0.00428 + 0.002 = \mathbf{0.00628}$$
Step 3: Heat Balance and Artificial Cooling
Rubbing velocity: $$V = \frac{\pi \cdot d \cdot N}{60,000} = \frac{\pi \times 60 \times 1200}{60,000} = \mathbf{3.77\text{ m/s}}$$ Heat generated: $$Q_g = \mu \cdot W \cdot V = 0.00628 \times 10,000 \times 3.77 = \mathbf{236.75\text{ Watts}}$$ Projected area: $A = \frac{90 \times 60}{10^6} = 0.0054\text{ m}^2$
Temperature difference: $(t_b - t_a) = \frac{1}{2}(60 - 16) = \mathbf{22^\circ\text{C}}$
Assuming ventilated bearing ($C = 1232\text{ W/m}^2/^\circ\text{C}$): $$Q_d = 1232 \times 0.0054 \times 22 = \mathbf{146.36\text{ Watts}}$$ Excess heat to be artificially removed: $$Q_a = Q_g - Q_d = 236.75 - 146.36 = \mathbf{90.39\text{ Watts}}$$ Mass of oil required (assuming standard $S = 1900\text{ J/kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C}$, $\Delta t = 10^\circ\text{C}$): $$m = \frac{Q_a}{S \cdot \Delta t} = \frac{90.39}{1900 \times 10} = \mathbf{0.00476\text{ kg/s} = 0.285\text{ kg/min}}$$
d = 60 mm, l = 90 mm | Q_g = 236.75 W | Q_d = 146.36 W | m = 0.285 kg/min
Q.4 Steam Engine Journal Bearing: Heat Generated & Dissipated (10 Marks)
Question: A Journal bearing is designed for a steam engine with following data:
Load on journal = 3 kN; Dia. of journal = 50 mm; Bearing length = 75 mm; Speed = 1600 rpm; Diametral clearance = 0.001 mm (ratio c/d = 0.001); Ambient temperature = 15°C; Oil SAE 10 is used for film temperature = 60°C; Absolute viscosity at 60°C = 0.014 kg/m-s. Determine heat generated and heat dissipated.
Step 1: Bearing Pressure ($p$)
$$p = \frac{W}{l \times d} = \frac{3,000}{75 \times 50} = \mathbf{0.80\text{ N/mm}^2}$$
Step 2: Coefficient of Friction ($\mu$) (Page 72)
Operating ratio: $\frac{l}{d} = \frac{75}{50} = 1.5 \implies k = 0.002$
Standard clearance ratio from book: $\frac{c}{d} = 0.001 \implies \frac{d}{c} = 1000$
Characteristic number: $\frac{Z \cdot N}{p} = \frac{0.014 \times 1600}{0.80} = 28.0$
$$\mu = \frac{33}{10^8}(28.0)(1000) + 0.002 = 0.00924 + 0.002 = \mathbf{0.01124}$$
Step 3: Heat Generated ($Q_g$)
Rubbing velocity: $$V = \frac{\pi \cdot d \cdot N}{60,000} = \frac{\pi \times 50 \times 1600}{60,000} = \mathbf{4.189\text{ m/s}}$$ Heat generated: $$Q_g = \mu \cdot W \cdot V = 0.01124 \times 3,000 \times 4.189 = \mathbf{141.25\text{ Watts}}$$
Step 4: Heat Dissipated ($Q_d$)
Projected area: $$A = \frac{l \times d}{10^6} = \frac{75 \times 50}{10^6} = \mathbf{0.00375\text{ m}^2}$$ Surface temperature difference: $$(t_b - t_a) = \frac{1}{2}(t_o - t_a) = \frac{1}{2}(60 - 15) = \mathbf{22.5^\circ\text{C}}$$ Case A (Ventilated bearing, $C = 1232\text{ W/m}^2/^\circ\text{C}$): $$Q_d = 1232 \times 0.00375 \times 22.5 = \mathbf{103.95\text{ Watts}}$$ Case B (Still air unventilated, $C = 280\text{ W/m}^2/^\circ\text{C}$): $$Q_d = 280 \times 0.00375 \times 22.5 = \mathbf{23.63\text{ Watts}}$$
Heat Generated Q_g = 141.25 W | Heat Dissipated Q_d = 103.95 W
Q.5 Derivation: Connecting Rod Buckling Strength $I_{xx} = 4I_{yy}$ (Summer-2026 Q.7b — 6 Marks)
In the X-X Plane (Plane of Motion / Crank Rotation):
The rod is free to rotate on the wrist pin and crankpin. Both ends act as hinged.
Effective length: $L_{ex} = L$
Euler's critical buckling load: $$P_{cr(x)} = \frac{\pi^2 \cdot E \cdot I_{xx}}{L^2}$$ In the Y-Y Plane (Perpendicular to Motion):
The ends are restrained from tilting by the long bearing bushes, acting as fixed ends.
Effective length: $L_{ey} = \frac{L}{2}$
Euler's critical buckling load: $$P_{cr(y)} = \frac{\pi^2 \cdot E \cdot I_{yy}}{(L/2)^2} = \frac{4 \pi^2 \cdot E \cdot I_{yy}}{L^2}$$ For Equal Resistance to Buckling in Both Planes:
Equating the two buckling capacities: $$P_{cr(x)} = P_{cr(y)} \implies \frac{\pi^2 E I_{xx}}{L^2} = \frac{4 \pi^2 E I_{yy}}{L^2} \implies \mathbf{I_{xx} = 4 I_{yy}}$$
Q.6 Steam Engine Flywheel Rim Design (Summer-2026 Q.8a — 15 Marks)
Single cylinder double acting engine: 150 kW at 80 rpm. Energy fluctuation Ce = 0.1. Speed fluctuation Cs = ±2% = 0.04. Mean rim dia = 2 m. Rim provides 95% of inertia. Cast iron density = 7200 kg/m³. Find rim mass and cross-section (b = 2t).
$$\omega = \frac{2 \pi (80)}{60} = 8.378\text{ rad/s}, \quad \text{Work done per cycle} = \frac{150,000 \times 60}{80} = 112,500\text{ J}$$ $$\Delta E_{\text{rim}} = 0.95 \times (0.1 \times 112,500) = \mathbf{10,687.5\text{ J}}$$ $$\Delta E_{\text{rim}} = m \cdot R^2 \cdot \omega^2 \cdot C_s \implies 10,687.5 = m(1)^2(8.378)^2(0.04) = 2.8076 \cdot m$$ $$m = \frac{10,687.5}{2.8076} = \mathbf{3,806.6\text{ kg}}$$ $$m = (\pi \cdot D \cdot A)\rho \implies 3,806.6 = \pi(2)(A)(7200) \implies A = \mathbf{0.0841\text{ m}^2 = 84,100\text{ mm}^2}$$ $$A = 2t^2 = 84,100 \implies \mathbf{t \approx 210\text{ mm}}, \quad \mathbf{b = 420\text{ mm}}$$
Rim Mass = 3806.6 kg | t = 210 mm | b = 420 mm
Unit II: Power Screws & Leaf Springs
Q.1 Horizontal Power Screw with Motor Drive (Summer-2026 Q.3b — 8 Marks)
An electric motor driven power screw moves a nut horizontally against 75 kN at 300 mm/min. Single square thread of 6 mm pitch on 40 mm major diameter. Thread friction = 0.1. Estimate power of the motor.
$$d_m = d - \frac{p}{2} = 40 - 3 = 37\text{ mm}, \quad \tan \alpha = \frac{6}{\pi \times 37} = 0.0516 \implies \alpha = 2.95^\circ$$ $$\tan \phi = 0.1 \implies \phi = 5.71^\circ$$ $$P_e = W \cdot \tan(\alpha + \phi) = 75,000 \times \tan(8.66^\circ) = 11,422.5\text{ N}$$ $$T = P_e \times \frac{d_m}{2} = 11,422.5 \times 18.5 = 211,316.3\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm} = 211.32\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$ $$N = \frac{300}{6} = 50\text{ rpm} \implies P = \frac{2 \pi (50)(211.32)}{60} = \mathbf{1,106.4\text{ W} = 1.11\text{ kW}}$$
Motor Power Required = 1.11 kW
Q.2 Broaching Machine Power Screw with Collar (Summer-2026 Q.3a — 12 Marks)
Broaching cutter pulled by square thread screw (d = 55 mm, p = 10 mm). Axial load = 400 N. Flat nut face D1 = 60 mm, D2 = 90 mm. Friction = 0.15 on all faces. Cutting speed = 6 m/min. Determine motor power and drive efficiency.
$$d_m = 50\text{ mm}, \quad \tan(\alpha + \phi) = \frac{0.0637 + 0.15}{1 - (0.15 \times 0.0637)} = 0.2157$$ $$T_1 = 400 \times 25 \times 0.2157 = 2,157\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}, \quad T_2 = 0.15 \times 400 \times 37.5 = 2,250\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}$$ $$T = T_1 + T_2 = 4,407\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm} = 4.407\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$ $$N = \frac{6000}{10} = 600\text{ rpm} \implies P = \frac{2 \pi (600)(4.407)}{60} = \mathbf{276.9\text{ W} = 0.277\text{ kW}}$$ $$\eta = \frac{W \cdot L_d}{2 \pi T} = \frac{400 \times 10}{2 \pi \times 4407} = \mathbf{14.45\%}$$
P = 0.277 kW | Efficiency = 14.45%
Q.3 Leaf Spring Design (Summer-2026 Q.4a — 12 Marks)
Total load = 120 kN on 4 springs. Span = 1000 mm. Max leaves = 10. Deflection = 80 mm. E = 200 kN/mm². Bending stress = 600 MPa. Find cross section.
$$W = \frac{120,000}{4 \times 2} = 15,000\text{ N}, \quad L = 500\text{ mm}$$ $$\frac{\sigma_b}{\delta} = \frac{E \cdot t}{L^2} \implies t = \frac{600 \times 500^2}{(200 \times 10^3) \times 80} = 9.38\text{ mm} \implies \mathbf{t = 10\text{ mm}}$$ $$\sigma_b = \frac{6 W L}{n b t^2} \implies 600 = \frac{6(15000)(500)}{10 \cdot b \cdot (10)^2} = \frac{45000}{b} \implies \mathbf{b = 75\text{ mm}}$$
Leaf Cross Section = 75 mm × 10 mm (10 Leaves)
Q.4 Proof: Self-Locking Power Screw Efficiency < 50% & Nipping Theory (10 Marks)
Self-Locking Efficiency Proof:
$$\eta = \frac{\tan \alpha}{\tan(\alpha + \phi)}$$ For self-locking: $\phi \ge \alpha \implies \alpha + \phi \ge 2\alpha \implies \eta \le \frac{\tan \alpha}{\tan(2\alpha)} = \frac{1 - \tan^2 \alpha}{2} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{\tan^2 \alpha}{2}$
Since $\tan^2 \alpha > 0 \implies \mathbf{\eta < 50\%}$.

Nipping in Leaf Springs:
Curving master leaves to a larger radius creates an initial gap at the center. Clamping induces pre-compressive stress in master leaves and pre-tensile stress in graduated leaves, equalizing final working stresses and preventing master leaf failure.
Unit III: Couplings & Keys
Q.1 Rigid Flange Coupling Design (Summer-2026 Q.6a — 15 Marks)
Two 35 mm shafts connected by flanged coupling with 6 bolts on 125 mm pitch circle. Transmits 800 N·m at 350 rpm. Stresses: Shaft shear = 63 MPa, Bolt shear = 56 MPa, CI flange shear = 10 MPa, Key shear = 46 MPa.
$$P = \frac{2 \pi N T}{60} = \frac{2 \pi (350)(800)}{60} = \mathbf{29.32\text{ kW}}$$ $$D = 2d = \mathbf{70\text{ mm}}, \quad L = 1.5d = \mathbf{55\text{ mm}}, \quad t_f = 0.5d = \mathbf{18\text{ mm}}$$ $$\tau_c = \frac{2T}{\pi D^2 t_f} = \frac{2(800,000)}{\pi(70)^2(18)} = 5.77\text{ N/mm}^2 < 10\text{ N/mm}^2 \implies \mathbf{Safe}$$ $$800,000 = \frac{\pi}{4} d_1^2 (56)(6)(62.5) \implies d_1 = 6.96\text{ mm} \implies \mathbf{\text{Adopt M8 Bolts}}$$ $$w = \frac{d}{4} = \mathbf{10\text{ mm}}, \quad h = \frac{d}{6} = \mathbf{8\text{ mm}}, \quad l = \frac{2(800,000)}{35(10)(46)} = \mathbf{100\text{ mm}}$$
Power = 29.32 kW | Hub: 70×55 mm | Flange tf = 18 mm | Bolts: 6 of M8 | Key: 10×8×100 mm
Q.2 Muff Coupling Design (Summer-2025 Q.6a — 10 Marks)
Transmitting 40 kW at 350 rpm. Stresses: Shaft & Key shear = 40 MPa, Crushing = 80 MPa, Cast iron muff shear = 15 MPa.
$$T = \frac{60(40 \times 10^6)}{2 \pi (350)} = 1,091,350\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm} \implies d = \sqrt[3]{\frac{16(1,091,350)}{\pi(40)}} = 51.8\text{ mm} \approx \mathbf{55\text{ mm}}$$ $$D = 2d + 13 = \mathbf{125\text{ mm}}, \quad L = 3.5d = \mathbf{195\text{ mm}}, \quad l = \frac{L}{2} = \mathbf{100\text{ mm}}$$ $$\tau_c = \frac{16(1,091,350)}{\pi(125)^3(1 - 0.44^4)} = 2.96\text{ N/mm}^2 < 15\text{ N/mm}^2 \implies \mathbf{Safe}$$
Shaft d = 55 mm | Muff: 125 × 195 mm | Key: 14 × 10 × 100 mm
Q.3 Key Types & Coupling Functions (5 Marks)
Four Types of Keys (Page 62):
1. Rectangular Sunk Key: $w = d/4, h = d/6$. Sits half in shaft and half in hub.
2. Gib-Head Key: Sunk key with an integrated raised head for extraction in blind assemblies.
3. Feather Key: Fastened to shaft with screws; allows hub to slide axially while turning.
4. Woodruff Key: Semi-circular disc sitting in curved keyway; auto-aligns on tapered shafts.

Coupling Functions & Applications:
Functions: Collinear torque transmission, accommodation of slight misalignments, vibration damping.
Applications: Motor to centrifugal pump, motor to gearbox, vehicle drivetrain to differential axle.
