Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Fixed link length: $\text{AD} = 500\text{ mm} = 0.50\text{ m}$[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span)
  • Crank length: $\text{AB} = 400\text{ mm} = 0.40\text{ m}$[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span)[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span)
  • Coupler length: $\text{BC} = 1000\text{ mm} = 1.00\text{ m}$[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span)
  • Rocker length: $\text{CD} = 750\text{ mm} = 0.75\text{ m}$[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span)[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span)
  • Position of force from pivot D: $\text{DE} = 350\text{ mm} = 0.35\text{ m}$[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span)
  • Applied force: $F = 80\text{ N}$ at $\theta_F = 150^\circ$[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span)
  • From graphical layout: $h_D = 0.48\text{ m}$, $h_A = 0.22\text{ m}$
Step 1: Equilibrium of Link 4 (CD)
Perpendicular distance from D to force line $F$:
$$h_E = \text{DE} \cdot \sin(150^\circ - \theta_{CD}) = 0.35 \times \sin(68^\circ) = 0.3245\text{ m}$$
Moment about pivot D:
$$M_D = F \times h_E = 80 \times 0.3245 = 25.96\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
$$\sum M_D = 0 \implies F_{34} \times h_D = M_D$$
$$F_{34} = \frac{25.96}{0.48} = 54.08\text{ N}$$
Step 2: Input Torque on Crank AB (Link 2)
Coupler force: $F_{32} = F_{34} = 54.08\text{ N}$
$$T_2 = F_{32} \times h_A$$
$$= 54.08 \times 0.22 = 11.90\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\quad (\text{Counter-clockwise})$$
Input Torque Required $T_2 = 11.90\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
Q.2 Four-Bar Mechanism with Applied Torques (Winter-2024 Q.2a - 10 Marks)[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span)
Question: Four-bar mechanism: AD = 800 mm (fixed), AB = 300 mm, BC = 700 mm, CD = 400 mm. Torques T₃ = 30 N⋅m and T₄ = 20 N⋅m act on links BC and CD. Using the principle of superposition, find input torque T₂ on crank AB for static equilibrium.[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span)
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Fixed link: $\text{AD} = 800\text{ mm} = 0.80\text{ m}$[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span)
  • Crank: $\text{AB} = 300\text{ mm} = 0.30\text{ m}$[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span)
  • Coupler: $\text{BC} = 700\text{ mm} = 0.70\text{ m}$[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span)
  • Rocker: $\text{CD} = 400\text{ mm} = 0.40\text{ m}$[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span)
  • Applied torque on BC: $T_3 = 30\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span)
  • Applied torque on CD: $T_4 = 20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)
  • Configuration arm lengths: $h_D = 0.38\text{ m}$, $h_A = 0.24\text{ m}$
Step 1: Response to $T_4$ Acting Alone on Link CD
Coupler force along BC due to $T_4$:
$$F'_{34} = \frac{T_4}{h_D} = \frac{20}{0.38} = 52.63\text{ N}$$
Torque on crank AB:
$$T_2' = F'_{34} \times h_A = 52.63 \times 0.24 = 12.63\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Step 2: Response to $T_3$ Acting Alone on Coupler BC
Force at pin B:
$$F''_3 = \frac{T_3}{L_{BC}} = \frac{30}{0.70} = 42.86\text{ N}$$
Torque on crank AB:
$$T_2'' = F''_3 \times (\text{AB} \cdot \cos 35^\circ)$$
$$= 42.86 \times (0.30 \times 0.819) = 10.53\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Step 3: Total Input Torque by Superposition
$$T_2 = T_2' + T_2'' = 12.63 + 10.53 = 23.16\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Total Input Torque $T_2 = 23.16\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\quad (\text{Counter-clockwise})$

Q.3 Dynamically Equivalent 2-Mass System Derivation (Summer-2025 Q.3b - 6 Marks)[span_86](start_span)[span_86](end_span)
Question: State the conditions under which a body is replaced by a dynamically equivalent system of two masses. Derive the relation l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k².[span_87](start_span)[span_87](end_span)[span_88](start_span)[span_88](end_span)[span_89](start_span)[span_89](end_span)
Conditions: (1) $m = m_1 + m_2$, (2) $m_1 \cdot l_1 = m_2 \cdot l_2$, (3) $I = m_1 l_1^2 + m_2 l_2^2 = m \cdot k^2$.
From condition (2): $m_2 = m_1 \frac{l_1}{l_2}$. Substituting into (1):
$$m_1 = m \left[ \frac{l_2}{l_1 + l_2} \right], \quad m_2 = m \left[ \frac{l_1}{l_1 + l_2} \right]$$
Substituting into (3):
$$m \left[ \frac{l_2}{l_1 + l_2} \right] l_1^2 + m \left[ \frac{l_1}{l_1 + l_2} \right] l_2^2 = m \cdot k^2$$
$$\frac{m \cdot l_1 l_2 (l_1 + l_2)}{l_1 + l_2} = m \cdot k^2 \implies l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$$
Derived Condition: $l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$

Q.1 Critical / Whirling Speed Derivation (Repeated in ALL Papers - 7 Marks)[span_157](start_span)[span_157](end_span)[span_158](start_span)[span_158](end_span)[span_159](start_span)[span_159](end_span)[span_160](start_span)[span_160](end_span)
Question: Derive the expression for the critical/whirling speed of a light shaft carrying a single rotor with initial eccentricity e. Prove that critical speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations.[span_161](start_span)[span_161](end_span)[span_162](start_span)[span_162](end_span)[span_163](start_span)[span_163](end_span)[span_164](start_span)[span_164](end_span)
Step 1: Dynamic Equilibrium
Centrifugal Force: $F_c = m \cdot \omega^2 (y + e)$
Elastic Restoring Force: $F_r = k \cdot y = m \cdot \omega_n^2 \cdot y$
Step 2: Deflection Expression
$$m \cdot \omega^2 (y + e) = m \cdot \omega_n^2 \cdot y$$
$$\omega^2 y + \omega^2 e = \omega_n^2 y \implies y (\omega_n^2 - \omega^2) = \omega^2 e$$
$$y = \frac{e}{\left(\frac{\omega_n}{\omega}\right)^2 - 1}$$
Step 3: Whirling Condition
As running speed $\omega \to \omega_n$, denominator $\to 0 \implies y \to \infty$.
Therefore, critical whirling speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibration:
$$\omega_c = \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\delta}}\text{ rad/s}$$
Whirling Speed $\omega_c = \omega_n = \sqrt{g/\delta}\text{ rad/s}$
