Q.5 Transfer Function: Definition, Significance & Properties (Winter-2024 Q.2b - 7 Marks)
Question: What is transfer function? State its significance and what must be the properties of transfer function.
Definition
The transfer function of a linear time-invariant (LTI) system is defined as the ratio of the Laplace transform of the output variable to the Laplace transform of the input variable, assuming all initial conditions are zero.
$$G(s) = \frac{\mathcal{L}\{\text{Output}\}}{\mathcal{L}\{\text{Input}\}}\Bigg|_{\text{initial conditions} = 0} = \frac{C(s)}{R(s)}$$
Significance of Transfer Function
1. Mathematical Model: It provides a compact algebraic representation of an LTI differential system in the complex frequency ($s$) domain.
2. System Independence: It is an inherent property of the system itself, entirely independent of the magnitude or nature of the applied input.
3. Poles and Zeros Analysis: The roots of the denominator (poles) determine transient stability, while roots of the numerator (zeros) dictate response shaping.
4. Impulse Response Equivalence: The inverse Laplace transform of the transfer function equals the unit impulse response: $g(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{G(s)\}$.
Properties of Transfer Function
1. Applicable strictly to Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems.
2. Defined only when all initial conditions are set to zero.
3. For physically realizable systems, the transfer function is a proper rational function (order of denominator $n \ge$ order of numerator $m$).
4. It does not provide any information regarding the physical nature of the system (electrical, mechanical, or thermal systems can have identical transfer functions).
Transfer function fully describes system dynamics in the s-domain independent of input excitation.
Q.6 Linear vs. Nonlinear Systems & System Classification (Summer-2025 Q.1a, Q.2a - 6 Marks)
Question: Distinguish between linear and nonlinear control systems. Discuss how control systems are classified.
Distinction: Linear vs. Nonlinear Systems
ParameterLinear Control SystemNonlinear Control System

Classification of Control Systems
1. Based on Feedback: Open loop (no feedback) vs. Closed loop (with feedback).
2. Based on Time Dependence: Continuous-time systems vs. Discrete-time (sampled data) systems.
3. Based on Parameter Variation: Time-invariant systems (constant parameters) vs. Time-variant systems.
4. Based on Number of Inputs/Outputs: SISO (Single-Input Single-Output) vs. MIMO (Multi-Input Multi-Output).
Superposition separates linear from nonlinear systems; feedback separates open from closed systems.
Q.6 Advantages & Disadvantages of Hydraulic Systems (Summer-2025 Q.4a - 6 Marks)
Question: List out the advantages and disadvantages of hydraulic systems.
Advantages of Hydraulic Systems
1. High Force Generation: Force levels can be made exceptionally high due to high operating fluid pressures ($70\text{ to }350\text{ bar}$).
2. High Power-to-Size Ratio: Compact actuators produce high torque and power compared to electric or pneumatic actuators.
3. Rapid Reversal & Smooth Operation: Motion can be reversed quickly and smoothly without mechanical shock.
4. Self-Lubricating: Hydraulic mineral oil continuously lubricates internal moving components and carries away heat.
5. Safe Overload Protection: Pressure relief valves protect the system against mechanical overload; when overloaded, actuators stall safely.
Disadvantages of Hydraulic Systems
1. Leakage & Cleanliness: Oil leakage creates dirty surroundings, slippery floors, and loss of efficiency.
2. Fire Hazard: Petroleum-based hydraulic oils are flammable when exposed to hot surfaces or sparks.
3. Temperature Sensitivity: Fluid viscosity changes with operating temperature, affecting system damping and response speed.
4. High Maintenance & Component Cost: Requires high-precision seals, continuous fluid filtration, and robust pumps.
Hydraulics deliver unmatched power density and stiff positioning, offset by leakage risks and higher maintenance.
Q.7 Pneumatic Proportional Controller (Summer-2026 Q.4b - 7 Marks)
Question: Sketch and explain the working of a pneumatic proportional controller.
Working Principle & Flapper-Nozzle Mechanism
A pneumatic proportional controller uses a flapper-nozzle amplifier paired with a negative feedback bellows. Regulated supply air ($P_s \approx 1.4\text{ bar}$) passes through an orifice to a nozzle. The error signal $e(t)$ deflects a flapper, changing nozzle back-pressure $P_c$.
1. When error $e(t)$ increases, the flapper moves closer to the nozzle, restricting airflow and causing output back-pressure $P_c$ to rise.
2. The rising back-pressure $P_c$ expands the feedback bellows, pushing the flapper back away from the nozzle (negative feedback).
3. Force equilibrium between input deflection and bellows displacement yields an output pressure directly proportional to input error:
$$P_c(s) = K_p E(s) \quad \text{where } K_p = \frac{b}{a} \cdot \frac{A_b}{K_b}$$
Output pressure $P_c(t)$ varies linearly with error $e(t)$ over standard $0.2\text{ to }1.0\text{ bar}$ ($3\text{ to }15\text{ psi}$) instrumentation ranges.
Q.7 Standard Test Signals in Time Response Analysis (Winter-2024, Winter-2025 - 6 Marks)
Question: Explain various types of standard test signals used in time response analysis with mathematical definitions and sketches.
Definitions & Laplace Transforms
1. Step Input (Displacement function): Represents sudden, instantaneous changes in input:
$$r(t) = A \quad (t \ge 0), \quad r(t) = 0 \quad (t < 0) \implies R(s) = \frac{A}{s}$$
2. Ramp Input (Velocity function): Represents signals that change linearly at a constant rate:
$$r(t) = A t \quad (t \ge 0), \quad r(t) = 0 \quad (t < 0) \implies R(s) = \frac{A}{s^2}$$
3. Parabolic Input (Acceleration function): Represents constant acceleration:
$$r(t) = \frac{A}{2} t^2 \quad (t \ge 0), \quad r(t) = 0 \quad (t < 0) \implies R(s) = \frac{A}{s^3}$$
4. Impulse Input (Shock function): Generates high energy over an infinitesimally short duration:
$$r(t) = \delta(t) \quad \text{where } \int_{-\infty}^\infty \delta(t)dt = 1 \implies R(s) = 1$$
Step tests position, ramp tests tracking velocity, parabolic tests acceleration, and impulse tests natural dynamics.

The denominator has no free integrator pole at the origin ($s^0$). Hence, the system is Type-0.

$$K_p = \lim_{s \to 0} G(s) = \lim_{s \to 0} \frac{50}{(1 + 0.1s)(s + 10)} = \frac{50}{(1 + 0)(0 + 10)} = \frac{50}{10} = \mathbf{5}$$

$$K_v = \lim_{s \to 0} s G(s) = \lim_{s \to 0} s \times \frac{50}{(1 + 0.1s)(s + 10)} = 0 \times 5 = \mathbf{0}$$

$$K_a = \lim_{s \to 0} s^2 G(s) = \lim_{s \to 0} s^2 \times \frac{50}{(1 + 0.1s)(s + 10)} = 0 \times 5 = \mathbf{0}$$

Q.4 Procedure for Construction of Bode Plot (Winter-2025 Q.9a - 5 Marks)
Question: State and explain the general procedure for construction of Bode plot.
Step-by-Step Construction Procedure
1. Convert to Time-Constant Form: Express $G(s)H(s)$ in standard Bode form: $G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(1 + j\omega T_a)\dots}{(j\omega)^N (1 + j\omega T_1)\dots}$.
2. Identify Corner Frequencies: Determine corner frequencies for each factor: $\omega_{c} = \frac{1}{T}$, and arrange in ascending order.
3. Determine Initial Magnitude Slope: Governed by type number $N$ (poles/zeros at origin): Initial slope is $-20N\text{ dB/decade}$. Point at $\omega = 1\text{ rad/s}$ is $20\log_{10} K$.
4. Construct Magnitude Table: As frequency increases past each corner frequency:
  • Pole $(1 + j\omega T)^{-1}$ adds $-20\text{ dB/decade}$ to current slope.
  • Zero $(1 + j\omega T)$ adds $+20\text{ dB/decade}$ to current slope.
  • Quadratic complex poles add $-40\text{ dB/decade}$.
5. Tabulate Phase Angle: Write expression for $\phi(\omega) = \sum \angle \text{Zeros} - \sum \angle \text{Poles} - N(90^\circ)$, compute values over frequencies spanning $\omega_{c,\min}$ to $\omega_{c,\max}$, and plot on semi-log paper.
6. Extract Margins: Read $\omega_{gc}$ (where magnitude $= 0\text{ dB}$) and $\omega_{pc}$ (where phase $= -180^\circ$). Compute $GM = -|G(j\omega_{pc})|_{\text{dB}}$ and $PM = 180^\circ + \phi(\omega_{gc})$.
Bode plot decomposes complex frequency response into asymptotic straight-line magnitude segments and phase curves.
Q.5 Bode Plot: Standard 3-Pole System $G(s) = \frac{10}{s(s+1)(s+10)}$ (Winter-2025 Q.9b - 9 Marks)
Question: For $G(s) = \frac{10}{s(s+1)(s+10)}$, state whether the system is stable or unstable using Bode plot.
Step 1: Time-Constant Form
$$G(s) = \frac{10}{s(s+1) \cdot 10\left(1 + \frac{s}{10}\right)} = \frac{1}{s(1 + s)(1 + 0.1s)}$$
Corner frequencies: $\omega_1 = 1\text{ rad/s}, \quad \omega_2 = 10\text{ rad/s}$. Gain factor $K = 1$.
Step 2: Magnitude & Phase Angles
Initial slope: $-20\text{ dB/decade}$ (Type-1). At $\omega = 1$: $|G|_{\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}(1) = 0\text{ dB}$.
Slope shifts to $-40\text{ dB/decade}$ at $\omega = 1$, then to $-60\text{ dB/decade}$ at $\omega = 10$.
$$\phi(\omega) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(\omega) - \tan^{-1}(0.1\omega)$$
Phase cross-over frequency occurs where $\tan^{-1}(\omega) + \tan^{-1}(0.1\omega) = 90^\circ$:
$$\omega_{pc} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T_1 T_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{1 \times 0.1}} = \sqrt{10} \approx \mathbf{3.16\text{ rad/s}}$$
At $\omega_{pc} = 3.16\text{ rad/s}$: Magnitude is $|G(j3.16)| = \frac{1}{3.16 \times \sqrt{1 + 3.16^2} \times \sqrt{1 + 0.316^2}} = \frac{1}{3.16 \times 3.31 \times 1.05} \approx \frac{1}{11} \implies \mathbf{-20.8\text{ dB}}$.
Gain Margin: $GM = -(-20.8\text{ dB}) = \mathbf{+20.8\text{ dB}}$. Since magnitude crosses $0\text{ dB}$ at $\omega_{gc} < 1\text{ rad/s}$ and $\omega_{gc} < \omega_{pc}$:
$\omega_{gc} \approx 0.8\text{ rad/s} < \omega_{pc} \approx 3.16\text{ rad/s} \mid GM = +20.8\text{ dB} \implies \text{System is STABLE}$
