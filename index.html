CORE CONCEPTS
Q2. State: (i) Principle of Superposition, (ii) Principle of Virtual Work, (iii) Two-force and Three-force member equilibrium.[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span)
1. Principle of Superposition: The total static response (internal forces, torques) caused by multiple external loads acting simultaneously equals the vector sum of the individual responses calculated by applying one load at a time[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).
2. Principle of Virtual Work: For any static mechanical system in complete equilibrium under frictionless constraints, the total virtual work done by all active forces and couples through an infinitesimal virtual displacement compatible with constraints is zero: ∑ Fi · δri + ∑ Ti · δθi = 0[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
3. Equilibrium Conditions:
  • Two-Force Member: The two forces must be equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and share the exact same collinear line of action passing through the pin centers[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).
  • Three-Force Member: The lines of action of all three forces must be concurrent (intersect at a single point O) and their vector sum must form a closed triangle (∑ F = 0)[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

Q4. Conditions for Dynamically Equivalent Two-Mass System of a Connecting Rod (Summer 2025, Winter 2025)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span)[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span)
To replace a rigid connecting rod of mass m, moment of inertia I, and radius of gyration k by two point masses m1 and m2 at distances L1 and L2 from the center of gravity, three dynamic conditions must be met:
  1. Mass Equivalence: m1 + m2 = m
  2. CG Equivalence: m1 · L1 = m2 · L2
  3. Inertia Equivalence: m1 · L12 + m2 · L22 = I = m · k2
Combining conditions (1) and (2) into (3) yields the condition:
L1 · L2 = k²
If mass m1 is fixed at the small end (wrist pin center), L2 = k2 / L1 determines the exact position of m2.

Q9. Prove that Whirling / Critical Speed of a Shaft equals the Natural Frequency of Transverse Vibration. (Summer 2026 Q9a, Summer 2025 Q9a)[span_54](start_span)[span_54](end_span)[span_55](start_span)[span_55](end_span)[span_56](start_span)[span_56](end_span)
Consider a light shaft rotating at angular speed ω carrying a disc of mass m. Let the center of gravity G be at an initial eccentricity e from the geometric center C. As it rotates, the shaft bends by deflection y from the bearing axis O.
  1. Centrifugal force acting outward at G: Fc = m · ω2 (y + e)
  2. Restoring elastic resistance force acting inward at C: Fr = k · y (where k is transverse stiffness)
  3. At dynamic equilibrium: k · y = m · ω2 (y + e)
  4. Rearranging for deflection: y (k - m ω2) = m ω2 e
    y = e · ω² / [(k/m) - ω²] = e · (ω / ωn)² / [1 - (ω / ωn)²]
  5. When running speed ω → ωn = √(k/m), the denominator becomes zero and deflection y → ∞ (severe resonance/whirling).
Therefore, critical whirling speed ωc = ωn = √(g / δ), which is identical to the natural frequency of transverse vibration[span_57](start_span)[span_57](end_span)[span_58](start_span)[span_58](end_span)[span_59](start_span)[span_59](end_span).
