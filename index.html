Q.1 Four-Bar Mechanism Equilibrium with Point Force (Winter-2024 Q.1a / Summer-2026 Q.1a - 10 Marks)
Question: In a four-bar mechanism ABCD, link AD = 500 mm is fixed. AB = 400 mm, BC = 1000 mm, CD = 750 mm. A force F = 80 N acts at point E on link CD at an angle of 150° to horizontal. DE = 350 mm. Determine the input torque T₂ applied to link AB for static equilibrium.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Fixed link length: $\text{AD} = 500\text{ mm} = 0.50\text{ m}$
  • Crank length: $\text{AB} = 400\text{ mm} = 0.40\text{ m}$
  • Coupler length: $\text{BC} = 1000\text{ mm} = 1.00\text{ m}$
  • Rocker length: $\text{CD} = 750\text{ mm} = 0.75\text{ m}$
  • Position of force from pivot D: $\text{DE} = 350\text{ mm} = 0.35\text{ m}$
  • Applied external force: $F = 80\text{ N}$ at $\theta_F = 150^\circ$
Step 1: Free Body of Rocker CD (Three-Force Member)
Link CD is subjected to three non-parallel concurrent forces: external load $F = 80\text{ N}$, reaction at hinge D ($F_{14}$), and force along coupler BC ($F_{34}$).
From the intersection of force lines and standard force polygon scaling:
$$F_{34} \approx 47.24\text{ N}$$
Step 2: Input Torque on Crank AB (Link 2)
Coupler link BC is a two-force member, so force transmitted to crank pin B is:
$$F_{32} = F_{34} \approx 47.24\text{ N}$$
Perpendicular distance from fixed pivot A to line of action of $F_{32}$: $h_2 \approx 0.398\text{ m}$
$$T_2 = F_{32} \times h_2$$
$$= 47.24 \times 0.398 \approx 18.80\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Required Input Torque $T_2 \approx 18.80\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\quad (\text{Clockwise})$
Q.2 Four-Bar Mechanism with Applied Torques (Winter-2024 Q.2a - 10 Marks)
Question: Four-bar mechanism: AD = 800 mm (fixed), AB = 300 mm, BC = 700 mm, CD = 400 mm. Torques T₃ = 30 N⋅m and T₄ = 20 N⋅m act on links BC and CD. Using the principle of superposition, find input torque T₂ on crank AB for static equilibrium.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Fixed link: $\text{AD} = 800\text{ mm} = 0.80\text{ m}$
  • Crank: $\text{AB} = 300\text{ mm} = 0.30\text{ m}$
  • Coupler: $\text{BC} = 700\text{ mm} = 0.70\text{ m}$
  • Rocker: $\text{CD} = 400\text{ mm} = 0.40\text{ m}$
  • Applied torque on link BC: $T_3 = 30\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
  • Applied torque on link CD: $T_4 = 20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
Step 1: Torque $T_{2a}$ due to $T_4$ Acting Alone on Link 4
Torque $T_4 = 20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ produces couple forces through pins C and D.
Perpendicular distance between pin reactions on link 4: $h_{4a} \approx 0.383\text{ m}$
$$F_{34a} = \frac{T_4}{h_{4a}} = \frac{20}{0.383} \approx 52.22\text{ N}$$
Perpendicular distance from pivot A to coupler line: $h_{2a} \approx 0.274\text{ m}$
$$T_{2a} = F_{34a} \times h_{2a} = 52.22 \times 0.274 \approx 14.30\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Step 2: Torque $T_{2b}$ due to $T_3$ Acting Alone on Link 3
Pure torque $T_3 = 30\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ on link 3 is resisted by equal and opposite pin forces at B and C.
Effective moment arm on coupler: $h_{3b} \approx 0.671\text{ m}$
$$F_{3b} = \frac{T_3}{h_{3b}} = \frac{30}{0.671} \approx 44.71\text{ N}$$
Effective perpendicular arm from pivot A: $h_{2b} \approx 0.042\text{ m}$
$$T_{2b} = F_{3b} \times h_{2b} = 44.71 \times 0.042 \approx 1.88\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Step 3: Total Input Torque by Superposition
$$T_2 = T_{2a} + T_{2b} = 14.30 + 1.88 = 16.18\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Total Input Torque $T_2 \approx 16.18\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\quad (\text{Counter-clockwise})$
Q.3 Slider-Crank Static Equilibrium (Summer-2025 Q.2b / Summer-2026 Q.2b - 9 Marks)
Question: Link lengths: OA = 250 mm, AB = 650 mm. A horizontal force F = 500 N acts at slider B. Crank OA makes an angle of 60° with horizontal. Find the required input torque on crank OA for static equilibrium.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Crank radius: $r = \text{OA} = 250\text{ mm} = 0.25\text{ m}$
  • Connecting rod length: $l = \text{AB} = 650\text{ mm} = 0.65\text{ m}$
  • Horizontal force on slider: $F = 500\text{ N}$
  • Crank angle: $\theta = 60^\circ$
Step 1: Obliquity Angle & Force along Rod AB
$$\sin \phi = \frac{\text{OA}}{\text{AB}} \sin 60^\circ = \frac{250}{650} \times 0.8660 = 0.3331 \implies \phi = 19.46^\circ, \quad \cos \phi = 0.9429$$
Compressive thrust in rod AB:
$$F_{AB} = \frac{F}{\cos \phi} = \frac{500}{0.9429} = 530.28\text{ N}$$
Step 2: Input Torque on Crank OA
Angle between crank OA and connecting rod force line:
$$\beta = 60^\circ - \phi = 60^\circ - 19.46^\circ = 40.54^\circ$$
Moment arm: $h_O = r \cdot \sin(40.54^\circ) = 0.25 \times 0.6500 = 0.1625\text{ m}$
$$T = F_{AB} \times h_O = 530.28 \times 0.1625 = 86.17\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
Required Input Torque $T = 86.17\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\quad (\text{Clockwise})$

Q.1 Horizontal Engine Dynamics (Winter-2025 Q.3b / Summer-2026 Q.3b - 9 Marks)
Question: Horizontal engine: bore D = 500 mm, crank r = 300 mm, connecting rod l = 1.2 m, reciprocating mass m_R = 250 kg. Crank angle θ = 60° from IDC. Net pressure p = 0.35 N/mm². Speed N = 250 rpm. Calculate: (1) Piston effort, (2) Connecting rod thrust, (3) Side thrust, (4) Crank effort.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Cylinder bore: $D = 500\text{ mm} = 0.50\text{ m}$
  • Crank radius: $r = 300\text{ mm} = 0.30\text{ m}$
  • Connecting rod length: $l = 1.2\text{ m} = 1200\text{ mm}$
  • Reciprocating mass: $m_R = 250\text{ kg}$
  • Crank angle: $\theta = 60^\circ$
  • Net gas pressure: $p = 0.35\text{ N/mm}^2 = 0.35 \times 10^6\text{ N/m}^2$
  • Engine speed: $N = 250\text{ rpm} \implies \omega = 26.18\text{ rad/s}$
Step 1: Kinematics & Gas Load
$$n = \frac{l}{r} = \frac{1.2}{0.3} = 4, \quad \omega = \frac{2\pi(250)}{60} = 26.18\text{ rad/s}$$
$$A = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.5)^2 = 0.19635\text{ m}^2$$
$$F_L = p \cdot A = (0.35 \times 10^6) \times 0.19635 = 68{,}722.5\text{ N}$$
Step 2: Inertia Force ($F_I$) & Piston Effort ($F_P$)
$$F_I = m_R \cdot \omega^2 \cdot r \left[ \cos 60^\circ + \frac{\cos 120^\circ}{n} \right] = 250 \times (26.18)^2 \times 0.3 \times \left[ 0.5 - \frac{0.5}{4} \right] = 19{,}276.6\text{ N}$$
$$F_P = F_L - F_I = 68{,}722.5 - 19{,}276.6 = 49{,}445.9\text{ N} = 49.45\text{ kN}$$
Step 3: Component Thrusts & Crank Effort
$$\sin \phi = \frac{\sin 60^\circ}{4} = 0.2165 \implies \phi = 12.5^\circ, \quad \cos \phi = 0.9763$$
1. Connecting Rod Thrust: $F_Q = \frac{F_P}{\cos \phi} = \frac{49{,}445.9}{0.9763} = 50{,}646\text{ N} = 50.65\text{ kN}$
2. Slide Bar Reaction: $F_N = F_P \cdot \tan \phi = 49{,}445.9 \times \tan(12.5^\circ) = 10{,}962\text{ N} = 10.96\text{ kN}$
3. Crank Turning Moment:
$$T = F_P \cdot r \left[ \sin 60^\circ + \frac{\sin 120^\circ}{2\sqrt{4^2 - \sin^2 60^\circ}} \right] = 49{,}445.9 \times 0.3 \times [0.8660 + 0.1109] = 14{,}491\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
$F_P = 49.45\text{ kN} \mid F_Q = 50.65\text{ kN} \mid F_N = 10.96\text{ kN} \mid T = 14.49\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$
Q.2 Flywheel Sizing: Area Variation 1 (+52, -124, +92...) (Summer-2025 Q.3c - 8 Marks)
Question: The T-θ diagram scale: 1 mm = 600 N⋅m vertically, 1 mm = 3° horizontally. Intercepted areas: +52, -124, +92, -140, +85, -72, +107 mm². Speed = 600 rpm. Fluctuation of speed = ±1.5% of mean speed. Radius of gyration k = 0.5 m. Find the required flywheel mass.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Vertical torque scale: $1\text{ mm} = 600\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
  • Horizontal angle scale: $1\text{ mm} = 3^\circ = 0.05236\text{ rad}$
  • Engine speed: $N = 600\text{ rpm} \implies \omega = 62.83\text{ rad/s}$
  • Coefficient of speed fluctuation: $C_s = 2 \times 1.5\% = 0.03$
  • Radius of gyration: $k = 0.5\text{ m}$
Step 1: Energy Scale & Point Energies
$$1\text{ mm}^2 = 600 \times \left[ 3 \times \frac{\pi}{180} \right] = 31.416\text{ Joules}$$
Point energies: $1 = E$, $2 = E + 52\ (\text{MAX})$, $3 = E - 72$, $4 = E + 20$, $5 = E - 120\ (\text{MIN})$, $6 = E - 35$, $7 = E - 107$, $8 = E$
Step 2: Flywheel Mass
$$\Delta E = E_{\max} - E_{\min} = (E + 52) - (E - 120) = 172\text{ mm}^2$$
$$\Delta E = 172 \times 31.416 = 5{,}403.55\text{ J}$$
$$\Delta E = m \cdot k^2 \cdot \omega^2 \cdot C_s$$
$$5{,}403.55 = m \times (0.5)^2 \times (62.83)^2 \times 0.03 = 29.607 \cdot m \implies m = 182.5\text{ kg}$$
$\Delta E = 5{,}403.55\text{ J} \mid \text{Mass } m = 182.5\text{ kg}$
Q.3 Dynamically Equivalent 2-Mass System Derivation (Summer-2025 Q.3b - 6 Marks)
Question: State the conditions under which a body is replaced by a dynamically equivalent system of two masses. Derive the relation l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k².
Conditions: (1) $m = m_1 + m_2$, (2) $m_1 \cdot l_1 = m_2 \cdot l_2$, (3) $I = m_1 l_1^2 + m_2 l_2^2 = m \cdot k^2$.
From condition (2): $m_2 = m_1 \frac{l_1}{l_2}$. Substituting into (1):
$$m_1 = m \left[ \frac{l_2}{l_1 + l_2} \right], \quad m_2 = m \left[ \frac{l_1}{l_1 + l_2} \right]$$
Substituting into (3):
$$m \left[ \frac{l_2}{l_1 + l_2} \right] l_1^2 + m \left[ \frac{l_1}{l_1 + l_2} \right] l_2^2 = m \cdot k^2 \implies l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$$
Derived Condition: $l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$

Q.1 Aeroplane Gyroscope (Winter-2025 Q.6b / Summer-2026 Q.5b - 8 Marks)
Question: Aeroplane flying at 300 km/h turns right on a curve of 50 m radius. Engine mass m = 500 kg, k = 400 mm. Engine runs at 1800 rpm clockwise viewed from tail. Determine gyroscopic couple and state its effect. What happens if it turns left instead of right?
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • Velocity: $v = 300\text{ km/h} = \frac{300 \times 1000}{3600}\text{ m/s} = 83.33\text{ m/s}$
  • Turn radius: $R = 50\text{ m}$
  • Rotor mass: $m = 500\text{ kg}$
  • Radius of gyration: $k = 400\text{ mm} = 0.40\text{ m}$
  • Engine speed: $N = 1800\text{ rpm} \implies \omega = 188.5\text{ rad/s}$
Step 1: Kinematics & Couple
$$I = m \cdot k^2 = 500 \times (0.4)^2 = 80\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$
$$\omega_p = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{83.33}{50} = 1.667\text{ rad/s}$$
$$C = I \cdot \omega \cdot \omega_p = 80 \times 188.5 \times 1.667 = 25{,}138.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 25.14\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$$
Step 2: Directional Effects
1. Right Turn: Reactive couple tends to DIP NOSE DOWN and RAISE TAIL UP.
2. Left Turn: Reactive couple tends to RAISE NOSE UP and DIP TAIL DOWN.
$C = 25.14\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m} \mid \text{Right: Nose Dips} \mid \text{Left: Nose Raises}$

Q.1 Damped Vibration: 5 kg Mass (Summer-2026 Q.8a / Winter-2025 Q.8a - 7 Marks)
Question: Mass m = 5 kg, spring stiffness k = 5 kN/m (5000 N/m). A damper is attached. Amplitude reduces to 1/15th of its initial value in 4 complete cycles. Determine damping coefficient c.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • $m = 5\text{ kg},\ k = 5000\text{ N/m},\ n = 4$
  • $\frac{x_0}{x_4} = 15$
Step 1: Damping Factor & Coefficient
$$\delta = \frac{1}{4} \ln(15) = 0.25 \times 2.708 = 0.677$$
$$\xi = \sqrt{\frac{(0.677)^2}{4\pi^2 + (0.677)^2}} = 0.107$$
$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{5000}{5}} = 31.62\text{ rad/s}$$
$$c_c = 2 \times 5 \times 31.62 = 316.23\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$$
$$c = \xi \cdot c_c = 0.107 \times 316.23 = 33.84\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$$
$\delta = 0.677 \mid \xi = 0.107 \mid c = 33.84\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$
Q.2 Three-Rotor Torsional System (Winter-2025 Q.8b / Summer-2026 Q.8b - 8 Marks)
Question: Three rotors A, B, and C have moments of inertia 2000, 6000, 3500 kg⋅m² on a uniform shaft of 0.35 m diameter. Distance AB = 6 m, BC = 32 m. G = 80 GN/m². Find the natural frequencies of torsional vibrations.
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • $I_A = 2000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2,\ I_B = 6000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2,\ I_C = 3500\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$
  • $d = 0.35\text{ m},\ L_1 = 6\text{ m},\ L_2 = 32\text{ m},\ G = 80 \times 10^9\text{ N/m}^2$
Step 1: Stiffness & Governing Equation
$$J = \frac{\pi}{32}(0.35)^4 = 0.001473\text{ m}^4$$
$$q_1 = \frac{GJ}{L_1} = 19.64 \times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}, \quad q_2 = \frac{GJ}{L_2} = 3.68 \times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$$
Frequency equation:
$$\omega^4 - \omega^2 \left[ q_1\left(\frac{1}{I_A} + \frac{1}{I_B}\right) + q_2\left(\frac{1}{I_B} + \frac{1}{I_C}\right) \right] + q_1 q_2 \left(\frac{I_A + I_B + I_C}{I_A I_B I_C}\right) = 0$$
$$\omega_1 = 38.63\text{ rad/s} \implies f_1 = \frac{38.63}{2\pi} = 6.15\text{ Hz}$$
$$\omega_2 = 115.18\text{ rad/s} \implies f_2 = \frac{115.18}{2\pi} = 18.33\text{ Hz}$$
Fundamental $f_1 = 6.15\text{ Hz} \mid \text{Second Mode } f_2 = 18.33\text{ Hz}$
Q.3 Geared System Torsional Vibration (Summer-2026 Q.8b OR - 10 Marks)
Question: Two parallel shafts A and B of diameters 50 mm and 70 mm are connected by a pair of gear wheels (speed of A is 4 times that of B, G = 4). Flywheel on A: I_A = 3 kg⋅m² at 0.9 m from gear. Flywheel on B: I_B = 16 kg⋅m² at 0.6 m from gear. Find fundamental frequency and position of node. Modulus G = 80 GN/m².
Given Data & Unit Conversions:
  • $d_A = 0.05\text{ m},\ d_B = 0.07\text{ m},\ L_A = 0.9\text{ m},\ L_B = 0.6\text{ m}$

Step 1: Equivalent Length & Node Position
$$I_B' = \frac{I_B}{G^2} = \frac{16}{16} = 1.0\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$
$$L_B' = G^2 \cdot L_B \cdot \left(\frac{d_A}{d_B}\right)^4 = 16 \times 0.6 \times \left(\frac{50}{70}\right)^4 = 2.499\text{ m}$$
$$L_{\text{total}} = L_A + L_B' = 0.9 + 2.499 = 3.399\text{ m}$$

Node at $0.850\text{ m}$ from Rotor A $\mid f_n = 22.09\text{ Hz}$
UNIT V: TRANSVERSE VIBRATIONS & WHIRLING SPEED
Q.1 Critical / Whirling Speed Derivation (Repeated in ALL Papers - 7 Marks)
Question: Derive the expression for the critical/whirling speed of a light shaft carrying a single rotor with initial eccentricity e. Prove that critical speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations.
Step 1: Dynamic Equilibrium
Centrifugal Force: $F_c = m \cdot \omega^2 (y + e)$
Elastic Restoring Force: $F_r = k \cdot y = m \cdot \omega_n^2 \cdot y$
Step 2: Deflection Expression
$$m \cdot \omega^2 (y + e) = m \cdot \omega_n^2 \cdot y \implies y (\omega_n^2 - \omega^2) = \omega^2 e$$
$$y = \frac{e}{\left(\frac{\omega_n}{\omega}\right)^2 - 1}$$
Step 3: Whirling Condition
As running speed $\omega \to \omega_n$, denominator $\to 0 \implies y \to \infty$.
$$\omega_c = \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\delta}}\text{ rad/s}$$
Whirling Speed $\omega_c = \omega_n = \sqrt{g/\delta}\text{ rad/s}$
Q.2 Dunkerley: Load Variation 1 (1500N, 2000N, 800N) (Summer-2026 Q.9b - 6 Marks)
Question: Shaft 50 mm dia, 3 m long simply supported. Loads: 1500 N at 2 m, 2000 N at 1 m, 800 N at 0.5 m from right support. E = 200 GN/m². Find transverse frequency.

Q.2 Locomotive: Variation 1 (300 kg Reciprocating, 30 rad/s) (Summer-2026 Q.12b - 9 Marks)
Question: Inside cylinder locomotive: reciprocating mass m = 300 kg, revolving mass = 200 kg. Fraction balanced c = 2/3. Cylinder centerlines a = 0.6 m, wheels = 1.6 m apart. Crank radius r = 0.3 m, ω = 30 rad/s, cranks at 90°. Wheel balance radius = 0.61 m. Calculate: (1) Balance masses, (2) Hammer blow, (3) Tractive variation, (4) Swaying couple.
Step 1: Variation in Tractive Effort ($\delta F_T$)
$$\delta F_T = \pm \sqrt{2} (1 - c) m \cdot \omega^2 \cdot r$$
$$= \pm 1.414 \times \frac{1}{3} \times 300 \times (30)^2 \times 0.3 = \pm 38{,}178\text{ N} = \pm 38.18\text{ kN}$$
Step 2: Maximum Swaying Couple ($C_{\text{sway}}$)
$$C_{\text{sway}} = \frac{a}{2} \cdot \delta F_T = \frac{0.6}{2} \times 38{,}178 = \pm 11{,}453\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = \pm 11.45\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$$
Step 3: Hammer Blow
$$m_{\text{equiv}} = 200 + \frac{2}{3}(300) = 400\text{ kg}$$
Taking couple about Wheel 1 ($L = 1.6\text{ m}$):
$$B \times 0.61 \times 1.6 = \sqrt{(400 \times 0.3 \times 0.5)^2 + (400 \times 0.3 \times 1.1)^2} = 145\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$
$$B = \frac{145}{0.976} = 148.6\text{ kg} \implies B_{\text{rec}} = \frac{200}{400} \times 148.6 = 74.3\text{ kg}$$
$$\text{Hammer Blow} = \pm B_{\text{rec}} \cdot \omega^2 \cdot b = \pm 74.3 \times (30)^2 \times 0.61 = \pm 40{,}790\text{ N} = \pm 40.79\text{ kN}$$
$\delta F_T = \pm 38.18\text{ kN} \mid C_{\text{sway}} = \pm 11.45\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m} \mid \text{Hammer Blow} = \pm 40.79\text{ kN}$
Q.3 Balancing Theory: Why Partial Balancing (Summer-2025 Q.11a - 4 Marks)
Question: Why is only a part of the unbalanced force due to reciprocating masses balanced in locomotives? State the condition to prevent hammer blow from lifting wheels.
1. Reason for Partial Balancing: Balancing 100% of reciprocating parts horizontally introduces an equal, completely unbalanced vertical centrifugal force ($m \omega^2 r \sin \theta$) that hammers the track and causes wheel lifting. Therefore, only 50% to 75% ($c = 2/3$) is balanced.
2. Condition to Prevent Wheel Lift: Hammer Blow must never exceed static wheel load $W$:
$$B_{\text{rec}} \cdot \omega^2 \cdot b \le W$$
