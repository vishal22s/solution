Q.1 Four-Bar Mechanism Equilibrium with Point Force (Winter-2024 Q.1a / Summer-2026 Q.1a - 10 Marks)
Question: In a four-bar mechanism ABCD, link AD = 500 mm is fixed. AB = 400 mm, BC = 1000 mm, CD = 750 mm. A force F = 80 N acts at point E on link CD at an angle of 150° to the horizontal. DE = 350 mm. Determine the input torque T₂ applied to link AB for static equilibrium.
Step 1: Equilibrium of Link 4 (CD)
Perpendicular distance from pivot D to force line F: h_E = DE ⋅ sin(150° - θ_{CD}) = 0.35 × sin(68°) = 0.3245 m
External overturning moment about D: M_D = F × h_E = 80 × 0.3245 = 25.96 N⋅m
Perpendicular distance from D to coupler line BC: h_D = 0.48 m
∑ M_D = 0 ⇒ F₃₄ × h_D = M_D ⇒ F₃₄ = 25.96 / 0.48 = 54.08 N
Step 2: Transmission to Crank AB (Link 2)
Force transmitted along BC to crank pin B: F₃₂ = F₃₄ = 54.08 N
Perpendicular distance from fixed crank pivot A to coupler line BC: h_A = 0.22 m
T₂ = F₃₂ × h_A = 54.08 × 0.22 = 11.90 N⋅m (Counter-clockwise)
Input Torque Required T₂ = 11.90 N⋅m
Q.2 Four-Bar Mechanism Equilibrium with Applied Torques (Winter-2024 Q.2a - 10 Marks)
Question: In a four-bar mechanism ABCD, fixed link AD = 800 mm, crank AB = 300 mm, coupler BC = 700 mm, rocker CD = 400 mm. Torques T₃ = 30 N⋅m and T₄ = 20 N⋅m act on links BC and CD respectively. By applying the principle of superposition, determine the magnitude and direction of the required input torque T₂ applied to link AB for static equilibrium.
Step 1: Response to T₄ Acting Alone on Link 4 (CD)
Rocker CD is subjected to torque T₄ = 20 N⋅m. Opposing couple is produced by pin reactions at C and D.
Coupler force along BC due to T₄: F'₃₄ = T₄ / h_D = 20 / 0.38 m = 52.63 N
Torque induced on crank link AB: T₂' = F'₃₄ × h_A = 52.63 × 0.24 m = 12.63 N⋅m
Step 2: Response to T₃ Acting Alone on Link 3 (BC)
Coupler link BC is subjected to pure torque T₃ = 30 N⋅m. Equal and opposite vertical forces at pins B and C:
Force at pin B: F''₃ = T₃ / L_{BC} = 30 / 0.70 m = 42.86 N
Torque induced on crank link AB: T₂'' = F''₃ × (AB ⋅ cos θ) = 42.86 × (0.30 × cos 35°) = 10.53 N⋅m
Step 3: Total Input Torque by Superposition
T₂ = T₂' + T₂'' = 12.63 + 10.53 = 23.16 N⋅m
Total Input Torque T₂ = 23.16 N⋅m (Counter-clockwise)

Q.1 Horizontal Engine Dynamics (Winter-2025 Q.3b / Summer-2026 Q.3b - 9 Marks)
Question: Horizontal engine: bore D = 500 mm, crank r = 300 mm, connecting rod l = 1.2 m, reciprocating mass m_R = 250 kg. Crank angle θ = 60° from IDC. Net pressure p = 0.35 N/mm². Speed N = 250 rpm. Calculate: (1) Piston effort, (2) Connecting rod thrust, (3) Side thrust, (4) Crank effort.
Step 1: Kinematic Values
n = l / r = 1.2 / 0.3 = 4;   ω = 2π(250)/60 = 26.18 rad/s
Area A = (π/4)(0.5)² = 0.19635 m² ⇒ Gas Force F_L = 0.35 × 10⁶ × 0.19635 = 68,722.5 N
Step 2: Inertia Force (F_I) & Piston Effort (F_P)
F_I = m_R ω² r [ cos 60° + cos 120° / 4 ] = 250 × (26.18)² × 0.3 × [ 0.5 - 0.125 ] = 19,276.6 N
F_P = F_L - F_I = 68,722.5 - 19,276.6 = 49,445.9 N = 49.45 kN
Step 3: Thrusts & Crank Effort
sin φ = sin 60° / 4 = 0.2165 ⇒ φ = 12.5° ⇒ cos φ = 0.9763
1. Connecting Rod Thrust: F_Q = F_P / cos φ = 49,445.9 / 0.9763 = 50,646 N = 50.65 kN
2. Slide Bar Reaction: F_N = F_P ⋅ tan φ = 49,445.9 × tan(12.5°) = 10,962 N = 10.96 kN
3. Crank Effort: T = F_P ⋅ r [ sin 60° + sin 120° / (2√(16 - sin²60°)) ] = 14,491 N⋅m
F_P = 49.45 kN | F_Q = 50.65 kN | F_N = 10.96 kN | T = 14.49 kN⋅m
Q.2 Petrol Engine Piston Effort: Small Bore Variation (Summer-2025 Q.4c - 8 Marks)
Question: Petrol engine: crank r = 50 mm, connecting rod l = 200 mm, cylinder bore D = 80 mm, reciprocating mass m_R = 1 kg. Speed N = 1800 rpm. When the piston has moved 10 mm from IDC, gas pressure p = 0.7 N/mm². Find: (1) Net load on gudgeon pin, (2) Thrust in connecting rod, (3) Cylinder wall reaction, (4) Engine speed at which these forces become zero.
Step 1: Kinematics at x = 10 mm
n = l / r = 200 / 50 = 4;   ω = 2π(1800)/60 = 188.5 rad/s
x ≈ r(1 - cos θ) ⇒ 10 = 50(1 - cos θ) ⇒ cos θ = 0.80 ⇒ θ = 36.87°, sin θ = 0.60
cos 2θ = 2 cos²θ - 1 = 2(0.8)² - 1 = 0.28
Step 2: Gas Force & Inertia Force
F_L = p × (π/4) D² = 0.7 × (π/4)(80)² = 3,518.6 N
F_I = m_R ω² r [ cos θ + cos 2θ / n ] = 1 × (188.5)² × 0.05 × [ 0.80 + 0.28 / 4 ] = 1,545.6 N
Net load on gudgeon pin: F_P = F_L - F_I = 3,518.6 - 1,545.6 = 1,973.0 N
Step 3: Thrusts & Zero-Force Speed
sin φ = sin θ / n = 0.60 / 4 = 0.15 ⇒ φ = 8.63° ⇒ cos φ = 0.9887
Thrust in connecting rod: F_Q = F_P / cos φ = 1,973 / 0.9887 = 1,995.5 N
Reaction on cylinder wall: F_N = F_P ⋅ tan φ = 1,973 × tan(8.63°) = 300.0 N
Speed for F_P = 0 ⇒ F_I = F_L ⇒ 1 × ω² × 0.05 × 0.87 = 3,518.6 ⇒ ω = 284.4 rad/s
N_0 = 284.4 × 60 / (2π) = 2,716 rpm
F_P = 1,973 N | F_Q = 1,995.5 N | F_N = 300 N | Zero-Force Speed = 2,716 rpm
Q.3 Flywheel Sizing: Area Variation 1 (+52, -124, +92...) (Summer-2025 Q.3c - 8 Marks)
Question: The T-θ diagram for a multi-cylinder engine has scale: 1 mm = 600 N⋅m vertically, 1 mm = 3° horizontally. Intercepted areas: +52, -124, +92, -140, +85, -72, +107 mm². Speed = 600 rpm. Fluctuation of speed = ±1.5% of mean speed. Radius of gyration k = 0.5 m. Find the required flywheel mass.
Step 1: Energy Scale

Step 2: Energy at Points
A = E;   B = E + 52 (MAX);   C = E - 72;   D = E + 20;   E = E - 120 (MIN);   F = E - 35;   G = E - 107;   H = E
Step 3: Flywheel Mass
ΔE = E_max - E_min = (E + 52) - (E - 120) = 172 mm² = 172 × 31.416 = 5,403.55 J
ω = 2π(600)/60 = 62.83 rad/s;   C_s = 2 × 1.5% = 0.03
ΔE = m ⋅ k² ⋅ ω² ⋅ C_s ⇒ 5,403.55 = m × (0.5)² × (62.83)² × 0.03 = 29.607 ⋅ m ⇒ m = 182.5 kg
ΔE = 5,403.55 J | Mass m = 182.5 kg

UNIT V: TRANSVERSE VIBRATIONS & WHIRLING SPEED
Q.1 Critical / Whirling Speed Derivation (Repeated in ALL Papers - 7 Marks)
Question: Derive the expression for the critical/whirling speed of a light shaft carrying a single rotor with initial eccentricity e. Prove that critical speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations.
Step 1: Dynamic Equilibrium
Centrifugal Force: F_c = m ω² (y + e);   Elastic Restoring Force: F_r = k y = m ω_n² y
Step 2: Deflection Expression
m ω² (y + e) = m ω_n² y ⇒ ω² y + ω² e = ω_n² y ⇒ y (ω_n² - ω²) = ω² e
y = e / [ (ω_n / ω)² - 1 ]
Step 3: Whirling Condition
As ω → ω_n, denominator → 0 ⇒ y → ∞ (Resonance). Hence: ω_c = ω_n = √(k / m) = √(g / δ) rad/s.
Whirling Speed ω_c = ω_n = √(g / δ) rad/s

Q.4 Balancing Theory: Why Partial Balancing (Summer-2025 Q.11a - 4 Marks)
Question: Why is only a part of the unbalanced force due to reciprocating masses balanced in locomotives? State the condition to prevent hammer blow from lifting wheels.
1. Reason for Partial Balancing: Unbalanced reciprocating forces act along the horizontal line of stroke ($m \omega^2 r \cos \theta$). To balance them, revolving counter-masses are added to the driving wheels. While these counter-weights balance the horizontal forces, their vertical component ($m \omega^2 r \sin \theta$) remains completely unbalanced, creating alternating upward and downward vertical forces (Hammer Blow). If 100% of reciprocating mass were balanced, the vertical impact on the track and wheel lifting would be severe. Therefore, only 50% to 75% ($c = 2/3$) is balanced.
2. Condition to Prevent Wheel Lift: Hammer Blow must never exceed the static wheel load $W$: $B_{\text{rec}} \cdot \omega^2 \cdot b \le W$.
