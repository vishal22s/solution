UNIT I: Static Force Analysis & Lubrication

Q1. (a) Differentiate between Thick Film (Hydrodynamic) and Thin Film (Boundary) Lubrication. (4 Marks)
(b) Differentiate between Hydrodynamic and Hydrostatic Bearings. (4 Marks)
(c) State the equilibrium conditions for: (i) Two-force member, (ii) Three-force member, (iii) Member with two forces and a torque. (4 Marks)
(a) Thick Film vs. Thin Film Lubrication
Characteristic Thick Film (Hydrodynamic) Thin Film (Boundary)
Film Thickness Thick film (10 to 100 μm); surfaces completely separated. Extremely thin molecular film (depth of surface asperities); partial metal contact occurs.
Coefficient of Friction (μ) Very low (μ = 0.001 to 0.03). High (μ = 0.05 to 0.15).
Governing Property Absolute viscosity of the bulk fluid (Z). Oiliness/chemical affinity of lubricant to metal surfaces.
Wear & Tear Practically zero during steady operation. Continuous surface wear occurs at peaks.
(b) Hydrodynamic vs. Hydrostatic Lubrication
Parameter Hydrodynamic Bearing Hydrostatic Bearing
Pressure Generation Self-generated by journal rotation wedging fluid into converging gap. Externally generated using a high-pressure pump before rotation begins.
Load at Zero Speed Zero load capacity at rest (metal-to-metal contact during starting). Full load-carrying capacity even at zero speed (no starting wear).
System Complexity Simple; no external pumps or high-pressure circuits needed. Complex; requires external pump, filter, relief valves, and accumulator.
(c) Static Equilibrium Conditions
  • Two-Force Member: The two forces must be equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and collinear (acting along the line joining their points of application).
  • Three-Force Member: The three forces must be concurrent (their lines of action meet at a single common point) or parallel, and their vector sum must equal zero (∑F = 0).
  • Member with Two Forces and a Torque: The two forces must be equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and non-collinear, forming a couple whose torque exactly balances the applied external torque (F × h = -T).
Q2. State and explain: (i) Principle of Superposition, (ii) Principle of Virtual Work. (4 Marks)
  • Principle of Superposition: In any linear mechanical system subjected to multiple external forces and moments simultaneously, the net resultant force or displacement at any point equals the algebraic or vector sum of individual effects calculated by considering each force acting alone while all others are removed.
  • Principle of Virtual Work: For an ideal mechanical system in static equilibrium without friction, the total virtual work done by all active external forces and couples during any arbitrary, kinematically admissible virtual displacement compatible with the system constraints is zero:
    ∑ δW = ∑ (Fi ⋅ δri) + ∑ (Tj ⋅ δθj) = 0

UNIT II: Engine Dynamics & Flywheels

Q1. (Numerical - Highly Repeated): The T-θ diagram for a multi-cylinder engine is drawn to a scale of 1 mm = 600 N⋅m vertically and 1 mm = 3° horizontally. The intercepted areas between the torque curve and mean resistance line are: +52, -124, +92, -140, +85, -72, +107 mm². The engine runs at 600 rpm. Total speed fluctuation must not exceed ±1.5% of mean speed. Find the mass of the flywheel if its radius of gyration is 0.5 m. (8 Marks)
Step 1: Energy Scale of 1 mm² Area
  • Torque scale: 1 mm = 600 N⋅m
  • Crank angle scale: 1 mm = 3° = 3 × (π / 180) = 0.05236 rad
  • 1 mm² Area = 600 × 0.05236 = 31.416 N⋅m (or 10π N⋅m)
Step 2: Total Kinetic Energy at Cycle Points

Let initial kinetic energy at cycle start = E.

  • Energy at A = E
  • Energy at B = E + 52
  • Energy at C = (E + 52) - 124 = E - 72
  • Energy at D = (E - 72) + 92 = E + 20
  • Energy at E = (E + 20) - 140 = E - 120 (MINIMUM ENERGY)
  • Energy at F = (E - 120) + 85 = E - 35
  • Energy at G = (E - 35) - 72 = E - 107
  • Energy at H = (E - 107) + 107 = E
  • MAXIMUM ENERGY occurs at point B = E + 52
Step 3: Maximum Fluctuation of Energy (ΔE)
ΔE = Emax - Emin = (E + 52) - (E - 120) = 172 mm²
ΔE in Joules = 172 × 31.416 = 5403.55 N⋅m (Joules)
Step 4: Solve for Flywheel Mass (m)
  • Mean speed: ω = 2πN / 60 = 2π(600)/60 = 62.83 rad/s
  • Coefficient of fluctuation of speed: Cs = ±1.5% &implies; Cs = 2 × 0.015 = 0.03
  • Radius of gyration: k = 0.5 m
ΔE = I ⋅ ω² ⋅ Cs = m ⋅ k² ⋅ ω² ⋅ Cs
5403.55 = m × (0.5)² × (62.83)² × 0.03
5403.55 = m × 0.25 × 3947.61 × 0.03 = 29.607 ⋅ m
m = 5403.55 / 29.607 = 182.5 kg
Q2. (Derivation/Theory): State D'Alembert's Principle and derive the conditions for a dynamically equivalent two-mass system of a connecting rod. (6 Marks)

D'Alembert's Principle: The vector sum of external active forces and internal inertia forces acting on a body in dynamic motion equals zero: ∑F - m⋅a = 0. It transforms a dynamic problem into an equivalent static equilibrium problem by introducing a fictitious reversed effective force (-m⋅a).

Dynamically Equivalent System (2-Mass System)

A rigid body of mass m, radius of gyration k, and center of gravity G can be replaced by two lumped masses m1 and m2 at distances l1 and l2 from G if and only if:

1. Total Mass Conservation: m1 + m2 = m
2. Center of Gravity Preservation: m1 ⋅ l1 = m2 ⋅ l2
3. Moment of Inertia Preservation: m1 ⋅ l1² + m2 ⋅ l2² = I = m ⋅ k²
Combining (1) and (2) into (3) yields the fundamental condition: l1 ⋅ l2 = k²

UNIT III: Gyroscopic Couples & Vehicle Dynamics

Q1. (Numerical - Repeated Verbatim): An aeroplane flying at 300 km/h turns right on a curve of 50 m radius. The mass of the engine and propeller is 500 kg with a radius of gyration of 400 mm. Engine speed is 1800 rpm clockwise viewed from the tail (rear). Determine the gyroscopic couple and state its effect. What is the effect if the aircraft turns left? (8 Marks)
Step 1: Convert Given Values to SI Units
  • Forward speed: v = 300 km/h = 300 × (1000 / 3600) = 83.33 m/s
  • Turn radius: R = 50 m
  • Engine mass: m = 500 kg; Radius of gyration: k = 400 mm = 0.4 m
  • Engine speed: N = 1800 rpm
Step 2: Angular Velocities & Mass Moment of Inertia
I = m ⋅ k² = 500 × (0.4)² = 80 kg⋅m²
ω = 2πN / 60 = 2π(1800)/60 = 188.5 rad/s (Spin velocity)
ωp = v / R = 83.33 / 50 = 1.667 rad/s (Precession velocity)
Step 3: Gyroscopic Couple (C)
C = I ⋅ ω ⋅ ωp
C = 80 × 188.5 × 1.667 = 25,138.4 N⋅m = 25.14 kN⋅m
Step 4: Physical Effect on Aeroplane
  • CASE 1: TURNING RIGHT Spin vector points forward (from tail to nose). When turning right, precession vector points vertically downwards. By the right-hand rule, the reactive gyroscopic couple tends to DIP THE NOSE DOWN and RAISE THE TAIL UP.
  • CASE 2: TURNING LEFT The direction of precession reverses. The reactive gyroscopic couple tends to RAISE THE NOSE UP and DIP THE TAIL DOWN.
Q2. Explain the gyroscopic effect on a Naval Ship during: (i) Steering, (ii) Pitching, and (iii) Rolling. (6 Marks)
  • 1. Steering (Turning in horizontal plane):
    • When the rotor rotates clockwise viewed from the stern (rear):
    • Steering to the Starboard (Right): Reactive couple tends to raise the bow and depress the stern.
    • Steering to the Port (Left): Reactive couple tends to depress the bow and raise the stern.
  • 2. Pitching (Up/down angular motion in vertical plane about transverse axis):
    • The motion is simple harmonic: θ = Φ sin(ω1t). Max precession velocity: ωp,max = Φ ⋅ ω1.
    • When bow rises: Reactive couple tends to turn the ship towards starboard (right).
    • When bow falls: Reactive couple tends to turn the ship towards port (left).
  • 3. Rolling (Rocking about longitudinal axis):
    Gyroscopic Effect during Rolling is ZERO.
    Reason: The axis of spin (propeller axis) and the axis of precession (rolling axis) are completely parallel/coincident. Since precession vector × spin vector = 0, no gyroscopic couple is generated.

UNIT IV: Longitudinal & Torsional Vibrations

Q1. (Numerical - Repeated Across 4 Papers): A mass of 5 kg is supported on a spring of stiffness 5 kN/m. A dashpot is attached whose resistance is proportional to velocity. The amplitude reduces to 1/15th of its initial value in 4 complete cycles. Determine the damping coefficient (force per unit velocity). (7 Marks)
Step 1: Given Data
  • Mass m = 5 kg
  • Stiffness k = 5 kN/m = 5000 N/m
  • Cycles n = 4; Amplitude ratio x0 / x4 = 15
Step 2: Logarithmic Decrement (δ)
δ = (1 / n) ⋅ ln(x0 / xn)
δ = (1 / 4) ⋅ ln(15) = 0.25 × 2.708 = 0.677
Step 3: Damping Ratio (ξ)
δ = 2πξ / √(1 - ξ²) &implies; ξ = √[ δ² / (4π² + δ²) ]
ξ = √[ (0.677)² / (4π² + (0.677)²) ] = √[ 0.4583 / (39.478 + 0.4583) ] = √[0.01147] = 0.107
Step 4: Critical Damping Coefficient (cc) and Damping Value (c)
ωn = √(k / m) = √(5000 / 5) = √1000 = 31.62 rad/s
cc = 2 ⋅ m ⋅ ωn = 2 × 5 × 31.62 = 316.23 N⋅s/m
c = ξ ⋅ cc = 0.107 × 316.23 = 33.84 N⋅s/m (or N/(m/s))
Q2. (Derivation): Derive the expression for the natural frequency of free longitudinal vibrations using the Equilibrium (Newton's) Method. (4 Marks)
Derivation:
  1. Consider a body of mass m suspended vertically from a spring of stiffness k. In static equilibrium, the extension is δst:
    W = m ⋅ g = k ⋅ δst
  2. Displace the mass downwards by an additional distance x from equilibrium and release it.
  3. Restoring force in spring = k(δst + x). Net accelerating force upwards:
    F = W - k(δst + x) = (k ⋅ δst) - k ⋅ δst - k ⋅ x = -k ⋅ x
  4. By Newton's Second Law: m ⋅ (d²x / dt²) = -k ⋅ x &implies; (d²x / dt²) + (k / m) x = 0.
  5. Comparing with standard SHM equation ((d²x / dt²) + ωn² x = 0):
    ωn = √(k / m) rad/s    fn = (ωn / 2π) = (1 / 2π) √(k / m) = (1 / 2π) √(g / δst) Hz
Q3. (Derivation/Formula): Derive the length of a Torsionally Equivalent Shaft for a stepped shaft. (4 Marks)

A stepped shaft consisting of lengths L1, L2, L3 with diameters d1, d2, d3 is replaced by a uniform shaft of diameter d and equivalent length Leq having identical total torsional stiffness (equal total angle of twist θ under torque T):

θ = θ1 + θ2 + θ3
T ⋅ Leq / (G ⋅ J) = [ T ⋅ L1 / (G ⋅ J1) ] + [ T ⋅ L2 / (G ⋅ J2) ] + [ T ⋅ L3 / (G ⋅ J3) ]
Since polar moment J = π d4 / 32, substituting gives:
Leq / d4 = (L1 / d14) + (L2 / d24) + (L3 / d34)
Leq = L1(d / d1)4 + L2(d / d2)4 + L3(d / d3)4

UNIT V: Transverse Vibrations & Whirling Speed

Q1. (Derivation - Repeated Every Year): Explain the concept of "Whirling / Critical Speed" of a rotating shaft. Prove that the whirling speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations. (7 Marks)
Definition & Physical Meaning

The whirling or critical speed is the rotational speed at which a rotating shaft becomes dynamically unstable, deflecting violently with excessive lateral bowing. At this speed, the rotational frequency matches the shaft's natural transverse vibration frequency, inducing destructive resonance.

Mathematical Derivation
  1. Let a disc of mass m be mounted on a light horizontal shaft.
    • O = Center of bearing axis
    • C = Center of geometric shaft cross-section
    • G = Center of gravity of disc
    • e = Eccentricity (distance CG)
    • y = Dynamic radial deflection of shaft centerline (distance OC)
  2. Total radial distance of CG from axis of rotation = (y + e).
  3. At shaft rotational speed ω, centrifugal force acting radially outward:
    Fc = m ⋅ ω² ⋅ (y + e)
  4. Restoring elastic force acting radially inward toward bearing axis:
    Fr = k ⋅ y = m ⋅ ωn² ⋅ y   (where stiffness k = m ⋅ ωn²)
  5. For radial dynamic equilibrium: Fc = Fr
    m ⋅ ω² (y + e) = m ⋅ ωn² ⋅ y
    ω² y + ω² e = ωn² y
    y (ωn² - ω²) = ω² e
    y = e ⋅ [ ω² / (ωn² - ω²) ] = e / [ (ωn / ω)² - 1 ]
  6. Condition for Whirling: As running speed ω approaches natural frequency ωn (ω → ωn):
    The denominator [(ωn/ω)² - 1] → 0 &implies; Deflection y → ∞ (Infinite Resonance)
  7. Therefore, critical whirling speed ωc is exactly equal to the natural frequency of transverse vibration:
    ωc = ωn = √(k / m) = √(g / δ) rad/s
Q2. State Dunkerley's Method for transverse vibrations of a shaft carrying multiple point loads and distributed shaft mass. (5 Marks)

Dunkerley's Empirical Formula: Estimates the fundamental natural frequency of transverse vibration (f) for a shaft supporting multiple point masses W1, W2, ... Wn plus uniformly distributed shaft weight Ws:

1 / fn² = (1 / f1²) + (1 / f2²) + (1 / f3²) + ... + (1 / fs²)

Where:

  • f1, f2, f3 = Natural frequencies considering each point load acting completely alone: fi = (1 / 2π) √(g / δi)
  • fs = Natural frequency of the uniformly distributed shaft self-mass alone: fs = (1 / 2π) √(g / δs), where effective static deflection δs = (5 / 384) ⋅ (w L4 / E I)

UNIT VI: Balancing of Masses & Locomotives

Q1. (Numerical - Standard 6-Column Method): Balancing of Several Masses Revolving in Different Transverse Planes. (9 Marks)

The 4 Golden Steps to Solve in the Exam Hall:

  1. Step 1: Choose Reference Plane (RP): Pick the plane of one unknown balance mass (say Plane A) as the RP so its distance lA = 0. This eliminates unknown mass mA from the couple calculations.
  2. Step 2: Construct the Universal Balancing Table:
    Plane Mass (m) [kg] Radius (r) [m] Cent. Force / ω² (m⋅r) [kg⋅m] Distance from RP (l) [m] Couple / ω² (m⋅r⋅l) [kg⋅m²] Angle (θ)
    1 m1 r1 m1r1 -l1 (left) -m1r1l1 θ1
    RP (A) mA (Unk) rA mArA 0 0 θA
    2 m2 r2 m2r2 +l2 +m2r2l2 θ2
    B mB (Unk) rB mBrB +lB mBrBlB θB
  3. Step 3: Solve Couple Polygon for Unknown B:
    ∑ Cx = ∑ (m ⋅ r ⋅ l) cos θ = 0
    ∑ Cy = ∑ (m ⋅ r ⋅ l) sin θ = 0
    Resultant Couple CR = √[ (∑Cx)² + (∑Cy)² ] = 0 &implies; Solve for mB and θB
  4. Step 4: Solve Force Polygon for Unknown A:
    ∑ Fx = ∑ (m ⋅ r) cos θ = 0
    ∑ Fy = ∑ (m ⋅ r) sin θ = 0
    Resultant Force FR = √[ (∑Fx)² + (∑Fy)² ] = 0 &implies; Solve for mA and θA
Q2. (Derivation/Formulas - Repeated Every Year): Explain partial balancing of reciprocating engines and write down the formulas for: (i) Variation in Tractive Effort, (ii) Swaying Couple, (iii) Hammer Blow. (8 Marks)

Why Complete Balancing is Impossible: Reciprocating masses introduce primary unbalanced forces along the line of stroke (m ω² r cos θ). If balanced completely with rotating counter-masses, an equal unbalance is introduced in the perpendicular (vertical) direction, causing rail lift. Hence, only a fraction 'c' (usually c = 2/3) of reciprocating mass is balanced.

1. Variation in Tractive Effort (δFT)

The net unbalanced force along the locomotive longitudinal line of motion produces an alternating pull/push:

δFT = ± √2 ⋅ (1 - c) ⋅ m ⋅ ω² ⋅ r
Max variation occurs at crank angles θ = 135° and 315°.
2. Swaying Couple (Csway)

The unbalanced forces acting along two parallel cylinder axes separated by distance 'a' create an alternating couple about the vertical axis, causing the engine to sway side-to-side (hunting motion):

Csway = ± [ a / √2 ] ⋅ (1 - c) ⋅ m ⋅ ω² ⋅ r = (a / 2) ⋅ δFT
Max swaying occurs at crank angles θ = 45° and 225°.
3. Hammer Blow (S)

The vertical component of the centrifugal force exerted by the rotating balance mass 'B' placed in the wheel at radius 'b' to balance reciprocating mass (c ⋅ m). It creates alternating impact on the rails:

Hammer Blow = ± B ⋅ ω² ⋅ b
Maximum downward pressure increases rail stress; maximum upward force can lift the driving wheel off the track if B ⋅ ω² ⋅ b > Static Wheel Load (P).
