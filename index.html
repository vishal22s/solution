Q.1 Four-Bar Mechanism Static Equilibrium (Winter-2024 Q.1a / Summer-2026 Q.1a - 10 Marks)
Question: The dimensions of a four-bar mechanism are: AD = 500 mm (fixed link), AB = 400 mm (crank), BC = 1000 mm (coupler), CD = 750 mm (rocker). A force of 80 N acts at point E on link CD at an angle of 150° to horizontal. DE = 350 mm. Determine the input torque T₂ to be applied on link AB to maintain static equilibrium.
Step 1: Equilibrium of Link 4 (CD)
Forces acting: External force F = 80 N at E, Pin reaction at D (F₁₄), and Coupler force along BC (F₃₄).
Perpendicular distance from D to line of action of F: h_E = DE ⋅ sin(150° - θ_{CD}) = 0.35 × sin(68°) = 0.3245 m
Moment of external force about D: M_D = F × h_E = 80 × 0.3245 = 25.96 N⋅m
Perpendicular distance from D to coupler line BC: h_D = 0.48 m
∑ M_D = 0 ⇒ F₃₄ × h_D = M_D ⇒ F₃₄ = 25.96 / 0.48 = 54.08 N
Step 2: Transmission to Crank AB (Link 2) & Input Torque (T₂)
Force exerted by coupler on crank pin B: F₃₂ = F₃₄ = 54.08 N
Perpendicular distance from fixed pivot A to coupler line BC: h_A = 0.22 m
T₂ = F₃₂ × h_A = 54.08 × 0.22 = 11.90 N⋅m (Anticlockwise)
Input Torque Required T₂ = 11.90 N⋅m

Q.3 Principles: Superposition, Virtual Work, Member Equilibrium (Summer-2025 Q.1a - 6 Marks)
Question: State: (1) Principle of Superposition, (2) Principle of Virtual Work, (3) Equilibrium conditions for two-force and three-force members.
1. Principle of Superposition: In any linear elastic system under multiple simultaneous loads, the resultant force or input torque equals the vector sum of individual responses determined by applying each load independently with all others removed.
2. Principle of Virtual Work: For a frictionless mechanical system in static equilibrium, the net work done by all active external forces during any arbitrary infinitesimal virtual displacement compatible with constraints is zero: ∑ δW = ∑(F_i ⋅ δr_i) + ∑(T_j ⋅ δθ_j) = 0.
3. Two-Force Member: Forces must be equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and collinear.
4. Three-Force Member: Lines of action must be concurrent (intersect at a single point) or parallel, and ∑ F = 0.

Question: Horizontal engine: bore D = 500 mm, crank r = 300 mm, connecting rod l = 1.2 m, reciprocating mass m_R = 250 kg. Crank angle θ = 60° from IDC. Net pressure p = 0.35 N/mm². Speed N = 250 rpm. Calculate: (1) Piston effort, (2) Thrust in connecting rod, (3) Side thrust on slide bars, (4) Crank effort.

n = l / r = 1.2 / 0.3 = 4
ω = 2πN / 60 = 2π(250)/60 = 26.18 rad/s
Piston area A = (π/4) D² = (π/4)(0.5)² = 0.19635 m²
Gas Force F_L = p × A = 0.35 × 10⁶ × 0.19635 = 68,722.5 N
Step 2: Inertia Force of Reciprocating Parts (F_I)

Step 3: Piston Effort (F_P) & Forces on Links
F_P = F_L - F_I = 68,722.5 - 19,276.6 = 49,445.9 N = 49.45 kN
sin φ = sin θ / n = sin(60°) / 4 = 0.2165 ⇒ φ = 12.5° ⇒ cos φ = 0.9763
1. Thrust in Connecting Rod: F_Q = F_P / cos φ = 49,445.9 / 0.9763 = 50,646 N = 50.65 kN
2. Side Thrust on Cylinder: F_N = F_P ⋅ tan φ = 49,445.9 × tan(12.5°) = 10,962 N = 10.96 kN
3. Crank Effort / Turning Moment: T = F_P ⋅ r [ sin θ + (sin 2θ / 2√(n² - sin²θ)) ]

Q.4 Dynamically Equivalent 2-Mass System Derivation (Summer-2025 Q.3b - 6 Marks)
Question: State the conditions under which a body is replaced by a dynamically equivalent system of two masses. Derive the relation l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k².
Conditions: (1) Total mass m = m₁ + m₂, (2) CG is unchanged: m₁ ⋅ l₁ = m₂ ⋅ l₂, (3) Moment of inertia I = m₁ l₁² + m₂ l₂² = m ⋅ k².
From (2): m₂ = m₁ (l₁ / l₂). Substitute into (1): m₁ [1 + l₁/l₂] = m ⇒ m₁ = m [l₂ / (l₁ + l₂)]
Similarly: m₂ = m [l₁ / (l₁ + l₂)]
Substitute m₁ and m₂ into (3):
m [l₂ / (l₁ + l₂)] l₁² + m [l₁ / (l₁ + l₂)] l₂² = m ⋅ k²
[l₁ l₂ (l₁ + l₂)] / (l₁ + l₂) = k² ⇒ l₁ ⋅ l₂ = k²

UNIT V: TRANSVERSE VIBRATIONS & WHIRLING SPEED
Q.1 Critical / Whirling Speed of Shaft Derivation (Repeated in ALL Papers - 7 Marks)
Question: Derive the expression for the critical/whirling speed of a light shaft carrying a single rotor with initial eccentricity e. Prove that critical speed equals the natural frequency of transverse vibrations.
Step 1: Dynamic Equilibrium of Forces
Total distance of Center of Gravity G from bearing axis O = (y + e).
Centrifugal force acting radially outwards: F_c = m ⋅ ω² ⋅ (y + e)
Restoring elastic force acting radially inwards: F_r = k ⋅ y = m ⋅ ω_n² ⋅ y   (since k = m ω_n²)
Step 2: Equating Forces
m ⋅ ω² (y + e) = m ⋅ ω_n² y ⇒ ω² y + ω² e = ω_n² y
y (ω_n² - ω²) = ω² e ⇒ y = e / [ (ω_n / ω)² - 1 ]
Step 3: Condition for Whirling
When running speed ω approaches natural frequency ω_n (ω → ω_n), denominator → 0 ⇒ y → ∞ (Infinite violent whipping).
Therefore, critical whirling speed: ω_c = ω_n = √(k / m) = √(g / δ) rad/s.
Whirling Speed ω_c = √(g / δ) rad/s = Natural Frequency ω_n

Q.3 Why Partial Balancing is Adopted in Locomotives (Summer-2025 Q.11a - 4 Marks)
Question: Why is only partial balancing of reciprocating masses adopted in locomotives? State the condition to prevent hammer blow from lifting wheels.
1. Why Partial Balancing: Unbalanced reciprocating inertia forces act horizontally (m ω² r cos θ). If 100% balanced by rotating counter-weights, an equal unbalanced vertical centrifugal force (m ω² r sin θ) is introduced. This vertical force causes heavy impact on rails and tends to lift wheels. Hence, only 50% to 75% (c = 2/3) is balanced.
2. Condition to Prevent Wheel Lift: Hammer blow must never exceed the static wheel load W: B ⋅ ω² ⋅ b ≤ W.
